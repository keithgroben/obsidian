# A Scriptural Framework for Navigating Church Fellowship, Discernment, and Separation

This document outlines a systematic, scriptural framework to address the perceived conflict between the command to "not forsake the assembling of yourselves together" (Hebrews 10:25) and the necessity of separating from churches that are doctrinally false, divisive, or spiritually ineffective.

The primary argument this framework refutes is the claim that a believer is "in sin" or "hindering their life" by not attending a local "church," even if all available options are spiritually damaging or "making the word of God of no effect" (Mark 7:13).

This framework argues that mere attendance is not the biblical standard. The biblical standard is the pursuit of holiness, doctrinal purity, and spiritual maturity within a true, Christ-honoring assembly.

## Part 1: The Foundational Premise – The Purpose of the Assembly

To understand the command in Hebrews 10:25, one must first understand the purpose of the assembly. The command is not arbitrary; it has a specific goal.

**The Purpose is Mutual Edification:** The preceding verse, Hebrews 10:24, defines the goal: "And let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works." The assembly is the context for this mutual encouragement and exhortation.

**The Purpose is Equipping and Maturity:** Ephesians 4:11-14 states that God gave pastors and teachers "to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ, until we all attain to... mature manhood... so that we may no longer be children, tossed to and fro by every wind of doctrine."

**Systematic Conclusion:** The biblical purpose of the assembly is to (A) stir believers to love and good works and (B) equip them with sound doctrine to produce maturity.

**Logical Implication:** An "assembly" that fails these functions—one that does not stir to good works but instead promotes error, or one that cannot equip but instead leaves believers as "children" tossed by false doctrine—has failed its primary biblical mandate.

## Part 2: The Command to Separate – Protecting the Assembly's Purity

The New Testament makes it clear that maintaining the purity of the church is a higher priority than maintaining unity with those who compromise it. Scripture explicitly commands separation in several clear cases.

**From Open, Unrepentant Immorality (1 Corinthians 5:11):** Paul commands believers, "you must not associate with anyone who claims to be a brother or sister but is sexually immoral or greedy, an idolater or slanderer, a drunkard or swindler. Do not even eat with such people."

- **Principle:** The holiness of the assembly must be protected from unrepentant sin. This establishes that separation is a divine command in certain contexts.

**From Divisive Persons and False Teachers (Romans 16:17):** "I appeal to you, brothers, to watch out for those who cause divisions and create obstacles contrary to the doctrine that you have been taught; avoid them."

- **Principle:** Doctrinal purity is paramount. Those who teach things "contrary to the doctrine" (i.e., anti-gospel, false gospels) are to be actively avoided.

**From Heresy (Titus 3:10):** "As for a person who stirs up division (a heretick in KJV), after warning him once and then twice, have nothing more to do with him."

- **Principle:** There is a clear, final process for dealing with those who insist on divisive, false doctrine.

**From False Teachers Seeking Fellowship (2 John 1:10-11):** "If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching [the true doctrine of Christ], do not receive him into your house or give him any greeting, for whoever greets him takes part in his wicked works."

- **Principle:** To "assemble" with a false teacher is not just neutral; it is to "take part in his wicked works." This makes attending such a church an act of disobedience, not obedience.

**Systematic Conclusion:** A believer is not obligated to assemble with so-called "brothers" who are unrepentant, divisive, or teaching a false gospel. In fact, a believer is biblically commanded to separate from them. This proves that Hebrews 10:25 cannot be a blanket command to attend the nearest building with a "church" sign on it, especially if that church is "anti-gospel" or a "cult."

## Part 3: The Command to Discern – Protecting Personal and Familial Maturity

This section addresses the more nuanced scenario: the sincere, but untrained or ineffective, teacher.

**The Principle of "Nullified" Power (Mark 7:13):** Jesus condemned the Pharisees for "making the word of God of no effect through your tradition." The result was a spiritually dead system. A teaching does not have to be "anti-gospel" to be dangerous; it can simply be a "gospel-nullified" teaching that is void of the transformative power it is meant to have.

**The Believer's Active Responsibility (1 Thess. 5:21 & Acts 17:11):** The New Testament never advocates for passive listening. The commands are active:

- "Test everything; hold fast what is good." (1 Thess. 5:21). This implies testing and letting go of what is not good.
- The Bereans were called "noble" because they "received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so" (Acts 17:11).

**The "Wood, Hay, and Stubble" Teacher (1 Corinthians 3:12-15):** This passage provides the clearest model for the sincere but ineffective teacher.

- They are building on the right foundation: "Jesus Christ" (v. 11).
- They are using poor materials: "wood, hay, straw" (v. 12). This represents teaching that is human-centric, based on denominational tradition, or simply untrained and shallow.
- The work will be "burned up" and "he will suffer loss," though he himself is saved (v. 15).

**Systematic Conclusion:** A believer has a direct, biblical responsibility to be a "Berean"—to test all teaching. If a teacher is consistently building with "wood, hay, and stubble," the believer is not obligated to remain and be "built" with materials that have no spiritual value and will be burned up. For the head of a household, this responsibility is magnified, as they are charged with the spiritual well-being of their family. It is an act of wisdom, not sin, to seek out a ministry that builds with "gold, silver, and precious stones" (sound doctrine, proper hermeneutics) that will last.

## Part 4: A Systematic Refutation of the Counter-Argument

This framework resolves the supposed conflict and provides a direct, "airtight" refutation of the counter-argument.

**The Counter-Argument:** "By not attending one of these three local churches, you are in sin, forsaking the assembly (Heb. 10:25), and hindering your family."

### The Systematic Refutation:

**This Argument Misapplies Hebrews 10:25.** It incorrectly defines "forsaking" and ignores the purpose of the assembly (v. 24).

- "Forsaking" (Greek: _enkataleipō_) means "to abandon" or "willfully desert." This describes a person with access to a healthy assembly who, through apathy or neglect, refuses to fellowship.
- This term does not describe a believer who, out of a high regard for the assembly's true purpose, makes a difficult choice to protect their family from spiritually dangerous or nullified teaching.
- In your scenario, two of the churches must be avoided per the commands of Romans 16 and 2 John 10. To attend them would be the actual sin.

**This Argument Creates a False Choice.** It forces a choice between (A) obeying Hebrews 10:25 (as they misinterpret it) and (B) obeying Romans 16:17, Titus 3:10, and 1 Thessalonians 5:21. A scriptural framework cannot be self-contradictory. The clear, unified command is to pursue pure fellowship, which requires rejecting impure fellowship.

**This Argument Elevates Attendance Over Purpose.** The counter-argument prizes the act of sitting in a pew over the purpose of being equipped. It demands you submit your family to a "wood, hay, and stubble" ministry (1 Cor. 3), which is a direct violation of the leader's duty to provide "solid food" (Heb. 5:14) and "equip" their family (Eph. 4).

### The Proposed Actions Are, In Fact, the Most Biblical Solution:

- **Phase 1 (Discernment):** You have "tested everything" (1 Thess. 5:21).
- **Phase 2 (Restoration Attempt):** You have attempted to "nudge" the pastor, fulfilling the spirit of Matthew 18 and Galatians 6:1.
- **Phase 3 (Temporary Assembly):** You are not forsaking assembly. You are assembling as a family unit (a "house church," see Philemon 1:2) and obeying the principle of Matthew 18:20 ("where two or three are gathered in my name").
- **Phase 4 (Seeking True Fellowship):** The plan to move is the single most powerful piece of evidence that you value the assembly, rather than "forsake" it. You are willing to make an enormous sacrifice to obey Hebrews 10:25 in its fullest, truest sense: to find a body of believers where you can "stir one another up to love and good works."

## Final Conclusion

The argument that you must attend one of these local churches is biblically indefensible. It ignores the explicit commands to separate from false and divisive doctrine, and it misunderstands the believer's responsibility to discern ineffective teaching. Your decision to protect your family, teach them at home, and actively plan to join a healthy, Bible-believing church is not an act of "forsaking" the assembly; it is an act of honoring it.
