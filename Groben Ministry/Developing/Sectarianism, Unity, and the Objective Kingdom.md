# Summary of Discussion: Sectarianism, Unity, and the Objective Kingdom

_A thorough, case-by-case summary of our conversation, structured as a document for your reference._

---

## Part 1: The Initial Observation (The Problem)

You identified a persistent and counter-productive pattern within your experience as a born-again evangelical Pentecostal Christian.

**The Phenomenon:** A "sliding scale" of correctness and animosity between denominations. You observed this in your own journey:

- Assemblies of God (AG) members looking down on Baptists and Nazarenes.
- Bethel members claiming a "better revelation" than the AG.
- This "tribal" ranking system creates division and competition.

**The Core Conflict:** You contrasted this with your understanding that the "pattern for the modern church" (based on the Book of Acts) should be judged by its **results**: spreading the gospel, discipling believers, fostering community, and being peaceable.

**The Foundational Realization:** You concluded that God is objective, and His Word (the New Covenant) has objective, covenantial properties. Therefore, the true measure of "rightness" is not one's denominational "claim" but one's adherence to this objective standard.

### The Core Questions:

- What is this phenomenon called?
- Why does it persist?
- What is the actual solution to it?

---

## Part 2: Defining the Phenomenon (The "What")

The behavior you described is identified as **Sectarianism**.

**Sectarianism:** An excessive or prejudiced devotion to a particular sect or denomination, which leads to animosity and competition against other groups who also identify as Christian.

**Underlying Attitudes:** This is fueled by:

- **Spiritual Pride/Elitism:** The "we're right and they're wrong" or "we have a better revelation" mindset.
- **In-Group/Out-Group Dynamics:** Using other denominations as an "out-group" to strengthen the identity of one's "in-group."

---

## Part 3: The Root Causes (The "Why")

_We explored why sectarianism is so pervasive, easy, and accepted, especially in the American context._

### Internal & Spiritual Roots

**Pride & Identity:** As you noted, it's tied to a "pride of wanting to be correct." A person's identity becomes so wrapped in their denominational brand ("God called me, I have a revelation") that a challenge to the brand feels like a personal attack.

**Fear:** A "fear of being wrong," which leads groups to build walls to "protect" the truth rather than bridges to share it.

**Misplaced Focus:** The focus shifts from the mission (the results from Acts) to the method (our specific traditions, worship styles, etc.).

### External & Cultural Roots

**A Consumer Mindset:** In America, churches are often treated as "products" in a religious marketplace. People "shop" for the church that best suits their preferences (music, kids' ministry, preaching style). This frames the "three churches on one street" not as a problem of division, but as a "virtue of choice."

**Normalization:** It's accepted because it's all many Christians have ever known. Denominationalism is the "water," so they don't see the "wetness" of the division.

**It's Easy:** Sectarianism is the path of least resistance. It requires no sacrifice. True unity is hard—it requires humility, sacrificing brand loyalty, and fellowshipping with those who practice differently. Tribalism is comfortable.

---

## Part 4: The Consequence (The "Why It Matters")

You correctly identified that the consequence of this division is that the Church becomes **"powerless and pretty mocked."**

It fails to produce the objective results of the Book of Acts and instead "just produces argument." The world, looking from the outside, doesn't see a unified Body focused on a single mission.

---

## Part 5: The Illustration of the Absurdity

_To highlight this absurdity, we used a real-world illustration:_

### The Plague and the Three Clinics

> **A city is dying of a plague (sin/lostness). A cure (the Gospel) is discovered.**
> 
> However, the doctors (churches) set up three different clinics on the same street, all possessing the exact same cure.
> 
> - **Clinic 1** insists the only right way to give the cure is with a blue syringe (e.g., Baptist tradition).
> - **Clinic 2** insists the only right way is with a red syringe (e.g., Pentecostal tradition).
> - **Clinic 3** insists syringes are heresy and it must be a white pill (e.g., another denomination).
> 
> **The Result:** While the city dies, the doctors are busy holding conferences, publishing papers on the "heresy of the pill," and poaching "healthy" members from each other's clinics. They are mocked not because their cure is ineffective, but because their division and pride render them ineffective.

### The Point:

**Focus on the Cure, not the syringe.**

---

## Part 6: The Solution (The "Pathway")

You established that the solution must be rooted in **objective correctness** while still allowing for a "celebration of diversity" (1 Cor 12: "a variety of administrations but one Lord"). The problem, as Paul noted in 1 Corinthians 1, is when we say "I follow Paul" or "I follow Apollos," which divides Christ.

The pathway for any Christian or church to move from sectarianism to a Kingdom mindset involves **three practical, objective shifts.**

---

### 1. The Shift in IDENTITY: From "Brand" to "Body"

|Sectarian View|Kingdom View|
|---|---|
|"I am a Baptist. 'Christian' is the category my brand fits into."|"I am a Christian. My 'administration' or assignment is within the Baptist tradition."|

**The Change:** This reframes other denominations from **competitors** to **colleagues**. The "Hand" stops seeing the "Foot" as a threat and recognizes it as a necessary part of the same Body.

---

### 2. The Shift in ACTION: From "Internal Debate" to "External Mission"

|Sectarian View|Kingdom View|
|---|---|
|"How can my church grow? How can we prove the other churches are wrong?"|"How can our three churches on this street together serve this city and show it the love of Christ?"|

**The Change:** This is the servant leadership you mentioned (Mark 10). It moves from **"lording over"** (my brand is best) to **"serving"** (my brand is irrelevant to the mission). When you are in the "trenches" of a shared mission, you stop caring about the logo on your fellow soldier's uniform.

---

### 3. The Shift in MEASUREMENT: From "Doctrinal Purity" to "Spiritual Fruit"

|Sectarian View|Kingdom View|
|---|---|
|"Are they 'right'? (i.e., Do they believe exactly what my tradition teaches?)"|"Are they objectively 'right'? (i.e., Are they producing fruit?)"|

**The Change:** This uses Jesus's objective standard: **"By their fruits you will know them"** (Matthew 7:16). The measure becomes:

- Do they love Jesus?
- Are they producing the "fruit of the Spirit" (Galatians 5)?
- Are they fulfilling the mission (the Acts results)?

If the answer is yes, they are on the same team, and their different "administration" is part of the Body's diverse strength, not a threat to its unity.
