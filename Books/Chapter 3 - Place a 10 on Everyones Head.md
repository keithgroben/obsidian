# Find a Way to Live in Unity and Peace

## THE SPARK

This is probably the subject you should pay attention to the most. The thing is, people, and maybe even yourself (though I'm not assuming) deal with this.
Complaining.
If you deal with this, it needs to end.
You're going to have problems. Problems with people. Problems with your denomination. Your board. The family that's been in your church for generations. Other churches in your city. People talking about you.
On the other side… you could be doing these things too.

## MY STARTING POINT (Backstory/Old Vehicle)

I had to go to these AG meetings every few years. They were far away. This usually resulted in a high cost of time and money. In those days of pastoring I didn't have the understanding of Bible prosperity. I was broke all the time. Our church was also broke.
Because severe lack was my reality, I reached out to the district office for help a few times.
Each time the answers were about the same. When I asked for goods they told me I could make the 452 mile drive (1 way) to see what they had available in their resource center. When I asked for money they didn't have any to give.
As you could imagine, that was not the help I wanted. I felt pain. Over time I became bitter. Then I complained, a lot.

## MY BIG DREAM (Desire)

A few weeks after I was saved as a middle school kid I wanted to be a Pastor. I believed God called me to it. I followed that call and ended up in one of the most remote towns on the continental US.
Pastoring a church requires a lot. And even though I attended two schools, had a degree, interned for 100s of hours and served in ministry in the fastest growing church in America as a volunteer for 8 years, I still didn't have everything I needed to succeed.
But my heart was in it, fully. I loved the Lord and knew I was serving Him and serving His people.
And for every dream or project we had as a church, there was always this reminder of how alone I was in the desert mountains and how seemingly unwilling the district was to help.

## HITTING THE WALL (The Wall/Conflict)

Every few years all the pastors in the district would go to a meeting with executives. I want to tell you briefly about two of them.
In traditional AG style, the meetings were very official and followed Robert's Rules of Order. At one point an executive who was also a pastor gave us a summary about how they had meetings to determine they would begin to reach out to and help us rural pastors. He did his best to give us something to look forward to as they continued to meet to determine what they could do.
This was hard for me to hear. It raked across the hurt and rejection I already felt. After the meeting they took us pastors to have a meal. After that we said our good byes.
On the way out the highest executive started talking to me. He asked how I enjoyed the church, the parsonage, and the city. I told him it was all great, which it was. I loved living there.
Then he asked me if I needed anything. So, I told him. While I was speaking he interrupted me like someone would a toddler. He raised his hand to stop me from talking.
He told me to contact the office and tell them. He turned around and started another conversation.
I about lost it.

## MY TURNING POINT (Epiphany/New Vehicle)

Three years had passed since our last meeting, and the bitterness from the district's rejection of my request for help had spread its infection through my family and church. However, for some unknown reason, I decided the day before the meeting that I would not let those feelings of isolation and lack overwhelm me.
My wife was surprised by my change in attitude as we drove to the meeting in Elko, Nevada. The weather was better than one of the previous times when I almost slipped off the snowy two-lane highway. In rural Nevada, a simple accident could leave you stranded for hours or even a day, with no cell phone coverage to call for help.
On the drive, I reinforced my decision to turn my bitterness into being better, remembering the early 2000s phrase, "Don't get bitter, get better." As I arrived at the church and climbed the stairs, I greeted the same senior-level executive in the foyer with a warm smile and friendly hello. He seemed surprised, not because he remembered me, but because he wasn't used to such warmth.
I complimented his smile and told him how encouraged I felt around him, causing him to blush and awkwardly groom my collar and tie. When he asked how everything was going, I replied, "Things couldn't be better," even though nothing had changed except my attitude.
The meeting continued with more of the same, but from that point on, I moved forward. I didn't need the district to change; I needed to change. It's easy to say, but I'm saying it from a place of struggling through it for five years before finally deciding to change inwardly, long before seeing any outward changes.
The journey to understand how to embrace the gospel and find prosperity for my soul was long, but it started with placing a ten on everyone's head. And it can start that way for you, too.
