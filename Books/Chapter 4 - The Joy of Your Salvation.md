# Chapter 4: The Joy of Your Salvation

## The Joy of the Lord is Your Strength

### Backstory: An Unexpected Mentor

Chicago Frank was everything you'd expect him to be.
I met him in Phoenix during one of the largest revivals in American history at Tommy Barnett's church, where around 350,000 people were saved in record time.
I arrived just as the revival was ending, and before starting Master's Commission or Phoenix First Pastor's College, I found myself playing basketball in the church gym alongside Mike Bibby and Matthew Barnett.
It was in this circle that I met Chicago Frank. As his name suggests, he was from Chicago and had a larger-than-life personality.
- There was a lot of mystery surrounding Frank.
- Was he in the mob?
- Where did he get the watches he sold?

But as I got to know him, I realized he understood something that the church at large didn't.
More importantly, he seemed to have a grasp on the prosperity of his soul that I certainly lacked at the time. Despite any spiritual or moral shortcomings he might have had as a new believer, Chicago Frank was very wealthy internally and externally and remained so even as his salvation grew.
It was a phenomenon, especially in a day when we were taught that having money meant compromising your spiritual life. Chicago Frank proved us all wrong.

### Desire: Seeking Prosperity

During my Bible college training days in Phoenix, I worked at a bank on Thunderbird Road. One day, a man who reminded me of Chicago Frank walked in - larger than life, high energy, and looking like a mobster.
True to the form you'd expect, the man walked in with two women on each arm, the type you'd see in a movie hanging on some gangster for his money. It seemed like a real-life version of that story.
He transacted something like $100,000, and out of curiosity, the teller asked where he got it. The man simply answered, "Investments," and smiled at us while we got the money from the safe and counted it out.
As we worked, he told the ladies on his arms, "You see, ladies, it's all energy. You just have to have the positive energy coming to you and sending the negative energy away. Anyone can do it."
The bank mobster, with typical movie-quality women on each arm, shared the same piece of information that Chicago Frank did. This was something I decided I really wanted, and I kept wanting it all the way until I was pastoring in Ely.
However, I had no clue what it actually was.
I translated that desire into zeal for God, rightfully so, but didn't understand how it would work in regards to my soul prospering and then creating the outward prosperity I wanted - the same outward prosperity I saw in these two people, one unsaved and the other saved.
As a pastor in my Nevada church, I desperately wanted to succeed. Despite the hardships and lack of money, I motivated myself by believing I was doing the right thing.
I determined that there was a bright future on the other side of my suffering and struggles, and I kept my attitude up beyond what was probably healthy, leading to cycles of depression in my life.
But my desire to succeed was so strong that I just kept going. I pushed through the difficulties, clinging to the belief that my efforts would eventually pay off. Little did I know that I was missing a crucial ingredient in my pursuit of success.

### Hitting the Wall: Struggles and Setbacks

As a pastor, I knew I needed a strength to keep me going in everything I was doing. I would start church growth projects or side businesses to get ahead financially, but every time I hit a roadblock, I would watch my plans crumble.
They would either:
1. Stop entirely
2. Be delayed far too long
3. Adapt into something less than my desired original outcome

Once, I remember going to God in prayer, sincerely asking for His help to make extra money using my skills as a graphic designer, web designer, and marketer. The Holy Spirit led me to start writing, but I faced the inevitable conclusion that I was not a writer and it would never work.
I didn't have the internal strength I needed to keep going.
At another time, I wanted the church to grow, so we started an adopt-a-block program that had proven successful for Tommy Barnett in Phoenix and Pastor Jeff Allway in inner-city ministry.
However, when I started the program:
- People in my congregation probably didn't understand it correctly
- I may not have given them the proper motivation or understanding myself
- When it became hard and faced resistance, I didn't have the strength to continue

This pattern kept happening to me, but because I wanted to succeed, I would make plans and eventually learned how to complete some of them while avoiding those that could fail.
Although this was wisdom, internally, there was something I kept hitting that caused me to fail.

### Epiphany: Discovering the Joy of the Lord

I wish I could say that I learned this lesson early on as a pastor, but the truth is, I found the solution I needed towards the end of my pastorate, and it solidified after I resigned.
As my time as a pastor was coming to a close, I began to tap into the energy that God gives through the Holy Spirit. I discovered that whenever you receive a revelation of Christ, the gospel, and the Holy Spirit's activity in your life, it becomes a strength that, when attached to the salvation you've received from the Lord, transforms into joy.
In the middle of a fight, the joy of the Lord becomes your strength. As the scripture says, "The joy of the Lord is your strength" (Nehemiah 8:10).

This is one of those lessons that I want to emphasize the importance of learning early on. No matter the challenge, conflict, or problem you face, you can get through it with sheer determination, but still lack the joy that strengthens you.
You often hear that you shouldn't do things in your own strength, but if you're like me, hearing something is very different from having someone explain to you mechanically how it works.
I can tell you firsthand that being in the high desert, in a very remote and isolated place, going through every type of issue, project, desire, and outcome – failing, changing my plans, succeeding, and being confused – and then learning how to navigate those things and make them happen, even when it was a hardship, there's not much personal reward in it. It can be a bit confusing.

However, when I learned to go through even harder things after being a pastor, things that perhaps the pastorate could have taught me or been the place to learn them, I discovered something profound.
I learned that going back to the joy of my salvation and really focusing on the gospel message is what gave me the joy of my salvation and the joy of the Lord.
From there, joy became my strength. I learned to put on joy in the same way you put on the mind of Christ and the same way you put on righteousness.
Joy is a revelation and a conscious decision that you make based on the understanding and reflection of the salvation that the Lord Jesus Christ has given you.
For me, it was a progressive revelation, from being a pastor to resigning and moving on to the next phase of my life, which you're witnessing now.
The only real strength comes from the continuous and deepening revelation of what Christ has done for me, time and time again.
