# Chapter 6: Community is Vital for Success

*Source: Notion — https://www.notion.so/dd5944185b5a406b9a519db804db6aaf*

### Introduction: Discovering the Power of Connection

Even when you're surrounded by people, you can feel completely alone. It's a paradox in the fast-paced, individualistic world.
Intentionally creating community transformed my life, ministry, and understanding of success.

### The Backstory: A Vision of Souls

I'll never forget the feeling when I stood on the deck behind my parents' house, just outside of Ely. It was elevated and I could see the entire city.
The town isn't big. There's just around 4,000 people. And at night I could see all the lights.
It had been a long time since I visited my parents due to a falling out a few years back. But through humility and love, we made things right.
We drove from Phoenix to visit them. The visit was great. All of the past problems faded away.
Standing on their back deck, I was happy to reconnect. Looking out at the peaceful scene, I'd soon realize there was much more connection that was coming.

### The Desire: Answering God's Call

At the time we had put a pause on serving at the church because of our new baby.
Even though we had served at Phoenix First for many years, it seemed that my desire for ministry was nowhere in sight.
That was until I saw something while standing on the deck that night.
From the deck I saw a vision of black smoke, coming from peoples souls. What looked like thin black smoke radiating hundreds of feet up into the sky. I knew in my spirit it represented the pain people were experiencing. That pain was reaching God.
I understood God was showing me this because He wanted somebody to reach out, love them, and preach the gospel.
I heard his voice asking me to help. I immediately said yes.
When we woke up the next morning we said our good-byes and started driving back to Phoenix. The vision I had stuck with me. The lack of scenery and nothing to look at made it easy for my wife to see something was on my mind.

### The Wall: Overcoming Doubts and Challenges

My intuitive wife asked me what I was thinking about not long after we headed down the road. I told her I didn't want to talk about it, wanting to pray and discuss it later.
She persisted, and I eventually said, "I think God's calling us to pastor the church there." Phoenix and Scottsdale are beautiful places with endless opportunities, so my wife's reaction was understandable: "Why would anyone move to Ely, Nevada?"
But she also loves the Lord with all her heart. Through a long, anointed conversation on the way back, we decided to pray, put it in God's hands, and let Him answer.
After all, God would have to do miraculous things, as we lacked the resources, money, or jobs to pick up and move, especially during a recession.
About a week and a half later, we got the confirmations we needed that this was indeed God's will. I worked at a high-end graphic design and video production studio in Scottsdale, which seemed unaffected by the recession.
If you didn't know, 2007 started the housing crisis and the global financial crisis.
Overnight, businesses stopped spending money. Larger companies made adjustments too.
The firm I worked at fired the cleaning lady, cut office staff hours, and let go of a graphic designer hired just two weeks after me.
One day, while working on a big project, I mentioned that I would be moving to Nevada to be a pastor soon. The owners were Christian. They asked when my last day would be.
I said a month, and they confirmed the date. I feel like I dodged being let go. I finished out my month, and then we moved.
Even though we were surrounded by people on all sides, the Phoenix felt so empty.

### The Epiphany: Finding Connection in Isolation

Holding onto all I had known for a long time—the big city and the megachurch—I found myself moving to rural Nevada, to Ely, a town of 4,000 people, and living 12 miles outside of that town in an even smaller town, McGill, with less than 900 people.
Finding a house to rent was nearly impossible. But a lady at the church my parents attended worked her relationships to find a house belonging to a doctor. He bought it to rent out when he came to work at the hospital.
We moved in. I went outside to enjoy some unseasonable snow and take a video but was interrupted by a gigantic semi driving down the one main road of the town.
This new chapter of my life would be so different—physically, spiritually, and with all my effort. I went from serving in a large ministry, inner-city evangelism, and many other things in Phoenix to moving to a small town, rekindling a relationship with my parents, and not knowing anyone else, just dreaming of pastoring that church.

**As we settled into our new life in Ely, I began to realize the true value of genuine community. The people in this small town looked out for one another, supported each other through tough times, and celebrated together in good times. It was a stark contrast to the isolation and individualism I had experienced in the big city and big church, despite being surrounded by people.**

What I learned is that in Phoenix, we were pretty much alone and left alone, despite serving in a big organization. Moving to Ely was made possible by my parents, who were completely different people, and by this lady in the church who represented an amazing quality that exists only in a small community like Ely—people genuinely caring for and supporting one another.
We worked that out for the decade we were there, and it became the greatest strength to our lives and the lives of others as we offered true, genuine community in a way that I don't think can exist outside of that setting.

**The unexpected family we found in Ely taught me that success isn't just about personal achievements or material wealth; it's about the connections we make, the lives we touch, and the love we share. This realization would go on to shape my ministry and my understanding of what it means to live a truly fulfilling life.**

What I found was connection in a remote, isolated place. It made the difference for our lives, our church, and it's something that has stayed with us all these years afterward. We genuinely love people and know how to stay in community with the ones we're called to be with.
