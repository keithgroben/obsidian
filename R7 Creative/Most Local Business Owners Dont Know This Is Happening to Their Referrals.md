## THE INSIGHT

"Here's what nobody ever tells you about referrals. A referral is the warmest lead you will ever get. Someone who already trusts you told another person your name. That person is already leaning toward yes before they've ever spoken to you.

But here's what happens in the gap between the moment they hear your name and the moment they actually call you. They look you up. Every single time. They open Google. They find your profile. They look at your reviews. They check if you have photos, any recent work, any sign that your business is active. And they spend about two seconds making a decision.

Now when I say low profit job — I mean the jobs that keep the lights on but don't move the needle. For a print shop that might be a single decal or a small banner. For a contractor that might be a small repair call. These aren't bad jobs. But they're not the jobs that change your business.

A more profitable job looks different depending on what you do. For a print shop it might be a commercial fleet wrap account — multiple trucks, recurring work, real money. For a contractor it might be a full renovation instead of a patch job.

The point isn't the size. The point is the trust required to get it.

People will gamble a few hundred dollars on someone they're not sure about. They will not hand over a commercial account — or a job worth thousands — until they trust you.

Positioning is how a potential customer feels about you compared to your competitor. You got two plumbers, both referred by the same person. One has 200 reviews and photos of every job. The other has 9 reviews, some from 2 years ago, months ago, even 2 weeks ago — sporadic, inconsistent. Which company will that person call?"

## POINT 1 — Your online presence is the second half of every referral

"I was talking to a roofer, I asked him what would be his ideal criteria for working with someone that could help him get more jobs. He said, 'well I love ideas.' I said 'what do you mean?' He said 'like for example I'm thinking about going to a barber, getting a cut, then asking him to give my card to every one of his clients when they come in and if they get a roof from me I'll give the barber a cut.' I said okay cool, now why are they going to call you? He said 'well… people trust their barbers.' I said right, the barber isn't delivering the service, you are. So why would they choose you compared to your competitor? He said 'yeah I get that, makes sense.'

People are going to call the company that looks like they can genuinely help the person that needs helped. The point isn't the reviews, content, SEO. The point is the trust required to get customers that pay more. All of that stuff is the method to get the customers you love working with the most.

From that, we looked at this print shop owner's profile. We filled out all of his information on Google. We entered every product and service he offers — so when someone searches for those things, Google recommends his business to them. We made sure everything was consistent and complete. Then we combined that with consistent reviews. When you do both of those things together — Google starts recommending you to more people who are actively searching for what you do.

That alone started changing what his referrals converted into."

---

## POINT 2 — Reviews determine how profitable the jobs you attract are

"Every job you finish without asking for a review is a missed shot at more profitable work.

Think about the last time you were looking for someone to hire. A doctor. A contractor. Anyone. You probably looked them up. You looked at their reviews. You looked at photos of their work. You wanted to know what other people experienced before you spent your money. Your customers are doing the exact same thing.

We gave this print shop owner a simple system. Call every customer right after the job while they're still happy about the result. Stay on the phone while they leave the review. That's the whole thing.

His review count grew. And the type of call he started getting changed. Because the level of trust his profile showed changed."

---

## POINT 3 — Content is a referral that never expires

"A referral lives inside one conversation between two people. It happens once and then it's gone.

Think about it like a restaurant. If someone tells their friend about a great place to eat — that conversation happens once and it's over. But a Google review of that restaurant stays up forever. Anyone searching for a place to eat in that city can find it — even people who never talked to that friend. That's what content does for your business.

Every photo of a completed job. Every before and after. Every video showing the work being done — it keeps working for you while you're out doing the job. People who never heard your name find you anyway.

So that's number three — content turns your reputation into something anyone can find. Not just the people who got the referral."
