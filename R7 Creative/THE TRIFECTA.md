## LEG 1: GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE

*Your new primary digital location — more important than your website.*

Your GBP is what shows up when someone searches your name, your industry, or "near me." Android does this natively, iOS users (Siri) rely on Google. All business aggregators like Yelp scrape Google Maps.

### What good looks like
| SET UP RIGHT | FED WEEKLY | EARNING TRUST |
|---|---|---|
| Every field filled out completely | Weekly posts (not updates — posts) | Reviews weekly — with UGC photos/videos |
| Correct categories (primary + secondary) | Weekly photo drops — real photos you took | QR code system for asking in person |
| Service areas defined | Weekly video drops — authentic, not polished | Human-written review replies (not AI) |
| All products listed | Google changed "updates" to "posts" | Every review liked and responded to |
| Business description with real keywords | | "Signs of life" — this is the trust signal |
| Hours accurate and kept updated | | |

### Common mistakes
- Setting it up once and never touching it again
- Using AI to write review replies
- Ignoring photos and video
- Not asking for reviews systematically
- Treating GBP as secondary to the website

## LEG 2: YOUR WEBSITE

*The credibility layer — where trust converts to action.*

### What good looks like
| FOUNDATION | CONTENT | CONVERSION |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile-first design | Real photos of your work, team, space | Clear call to action on every page |
| Fast loading — under 3 seconds | Clear service description with location keywords | Phone number clickable on mobile |
| SSL certificate (https) | About page — who you are, why you do this | Contact form that actually works |
| Consistent NAP matching GBP | Blog/content section | Google Maps embed with your location |
| Clean navigation — 5 pages max | Testimonials from real customers | Hours visible without hunting |

**Big insight:** Google Business Profile support docs say that if you fully complete your GBP and 'match and mirror' it to your website properly, they will promote BOTH more.

### Common mistakes
- Spending $10K on a fancy website but never updating it
- No mobile optimization
- Stock photos everywhere
- Burying the phone number
- Set-it-and-forget-it treatment

**For churches:** your website is how first-time visitors decide whether to show up Sunday. Service times, what to expect, who the pastor is, what the church believes.

## LEG 3: SOCIAL MEDIA CONTENT

*The amplifier — signs of life that build trust everywhere else.*

Organic reach on Meta/Tiktok is functionally dead unless you pay. But social is where people go to validate that you're real, active, and trustworthy.

### What good looks like
| THE STRATEGY | THE CONTENT | THE PLATFORMS THAT MATTER |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business first, social supporting | Real photos and videos of your work | YouTube — still has organic reach |
| Same authentic content posted to both | Behind-the-scenes | Often the #1 click result on Google |
| You capture, we create | Customer stories, wins | Facebook/Instagram/TikTok — pay-to-play |
| Consistency beats perfection | Team and culture content | Still useful for social proof |
| Better than a few local competitors = you win | Short-form video (under 60s) | TikTok — unstable |
| Make content, don't overthink it | Authentic > polished | Don't build on rented land — link in bio to contact capture |

### Common mistakes
- Trying to be on every platform natively (use COPE: create once, post everywhere)
- Only posting when you have something to sell
- Hiring someone to post generic AI/Canva content
- Expecting organic reach
- Ignoring YouTube
- Chasing viral — social is validation, not discovery
