For Interview Transcript Workflow (Updated with Form Integration)

## Workflow Overview
For clients who:
1. Complete a preliminary Tally form with 5 strategic questions
2. Participate in a 30-45 minute recorded interview using 10 strategic questions
3. Receive Brand Pillars built from both sources

### Your Process
1. Client completes Tally form (before interview)
2. Analyze form responses using Pre-Interview Form Analysis Guide
3. Conduct interview (customized based on form gaps)
4. Read and analyze interview transcript
5. Combine form + transcript material
6. Build the 6 Brand Pillars
7. Get approval and make revisions
8. Create Marketing Message Examples

---

## The Tally Form Questions (Pre-Interview)

1. Name / Business / Email
2. "List your main services/products and the types of clients you work with most" — *feeds: Brand Positioning, Target Market*
3. "Name 2-3 actual clients from the past year that represent your IDEAL customer" — *feeds: Target Market, Customer Avatar (use these real names throughout)*
4. "What type of work do you want MORE of, and what do you want LESS of?" — *feeds: Brand Positioning, Target Market*
5. "Tell me about one recent project you're proud of" — *feeds: UVP, Problem Solved, Story material*

## The 10 Interview Questions

### Part 1: Focus & Positioning (Q1-3)
1. Type of work you WANT more of vs. what you wish you could stop doing
2. Favorite client/project from last year and why
3. Situation your ideal client is in when they realize they need you

### Part 2: Problems & Transformation (Q4-6)
4. Walk through a recent project start to finish — what client wanted, what you delivered, what changed
5. Cost to clients if they DON'T do this — not just money, what else suffers
6. What clients DON'T understand until after working with you — the "aha moment"

### Part 3: Differentiation (Q7-8)
7. Why do your best clients choose you over other options?
8. Something you do as part of normal process that competitors skip or charge extra for

### Part 4: Objections & Messaging (Q9-10)
9. Biggest hesitation/objection before someone decides to work with you
10. If you could make your ideal client deeply understand ONE thing about working with you

## Step 0: Pre-Interview Analysis

Before conducting interview:
1. Review Form Answers Quality (which got specific vs. vague answers?)
2. Pre-Map to Pillars (what material extractable from strong form answers?)
3. Extract Key Elements (client names, services focus, MORE/LESS, proud project)
4. Prepare Interview Focus Areas (3-5 areas where form was weak)

### Form Question → Pillar Mapping
- "Main services/client types" → Brand Positioning, Target Market
- "2-3 ideal clients" → Target Market, Customer Avatar (base persona on these)
- "Want MORE/LESS work" → Brand Positioning, Target Market
- "Recent proud project" → UVP, Problem Solved (transformation story)

---

## Step 1: Analyze the Transcript

### Quality Markers (Good Responses)
- **Specificity:** Named clients, numbers and timeframes, concrete details, real examples
- **Customer Perspective:** What clients SAY, decision factors, transformation language, quotes
- **Authentic Voice:** Industry insider language, conversational tone, opinions, natural stories
- **Clear Positioning:** MORE vs. LESS, specific client types, operational differentiation

### Red Flags (Weak Responses)
- **Vagueness:** "We serve small businesses," "We provide quality," "Clients love us"
- **Features not benefits:** Long descriptions of WHAT vs. what changes for clients
- **No examples:** Theoretical answers, generalizations, claims without proof
- **Missing customer voice:** No mention of what clients say, no objections, no before/after
- **Contradictions:** Says they serve everyone but describes specific ideal client

---

## Step 2: Combine Form + Transcript Analysis

**Form typically provides well:** Client names and examples, positioning direction (MORE/LESS), concise service description, starting transformation story

**Interview typically provides well:** Detailed stories and context, authentic voice, differentiation and unique process, customer objections and aha moments, emotional weight

### Strategy
- Use form for anchors: client names, positioning direction, key examples
- Use interview for depth: stories, differentiation, authentic voice, details
- Reference form examples, expand with interview stories
- Prioritize strongest source

## Step 4: Build the 6 Brand Pillars

### 1. Brand Positioning Statement (100-150 words)
**Opening:** "[Business Name]: [One compelling sentence]"
Follow with 4-5 bullets: distinct space vs. competitors, philosophical approach, what they deliberately do differently, industry conventions they reject, specific client type served best.
**Draw from:** Form Q2, Q4 (MORE/LESS); Interview Q1, Q7, Q8

### 2. Unique Value Proposition (100-150 words)
**Opening:** One sentence (60 words max) capturing primary transformation.
Follow with 4-5 bullets: specific transformation, unique delivery, what clients don't realize until after, why competitors can't easily copy, proof example.
**Draw from:** Form Q5; Interview Q4, Q6, Q8, Q10

### 3. Target Market Definition (100-150 words)
**CRITICAL: Use real client names from form Q3.**
2-3 opening sentences describing who they serve (use actual examples).
Follow with bullets: situation/circumstances, industry/business type, primary pain points, decision triggers, what they value enough to pay for.
**Draw from:** Form Q2, Q3, Q4; Interview Q2, Q3, Q7

### 4. Problem Solved Statement (100-150 words)
2-3 sentences articulating the core problem. 3-4 bullets on consequences if unsolved. 2-3 bullets on transformation created. Frame as "from X to Y."
**Draw from:** Form Q5; Interview Q3, Q4, Q5

### 5. Customer Avatar Summary (100-150 words)
**CRITICAL: Base on actual clients from form Q3.**
"Based on the ideal clients you named — [Client 1], [Client 2], [Client 3] — here's your customer avatar: Meet [Name], owner of [matches pattern]..."
Follow with 5-6 bullets: emotional state, daily frustrations, what they've tried, objections/concerns, what success looks like, decision factors.
**Draw from:** Form Q3 ← ANCHOR; Interview Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q9

### 6. Messaging Framework (100-150 words)
3-5 key messages + tone & voice + core differentiation to emphasize.
**Draw from:** Form Q4; Interview Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10 + entire transcript

## Critical Quality Standards

### Every pillar must:
- Be 100-150 words
- Include specific details from form AND transcript
- Use client's authentic language
- Reference actual examples (especially form Q3 client names)
- Differentiate with specifics
- Focus on customer outcomes
- Avoid generic claims

### Red flags that mean revision needed:
- Could apply to any business in their industry
- No specific examples or proof
- Full of buzzwords they never said
- No reference to clients/projects they mentioned
- Reads like AI wrote it for AI
- Client says "this doesn't sound like us"

### Test each pillar:
- Point to 2-3 specific form + transcript moments that support this?
- Uses language/phrases they actually said or wrote?
- References real clients they named?
- Would competitors' pillars say something different?

---

## Handling Weak Material

If both form AND transcript have weak answers, be direct:

"I've reviewed your intake form and interview transcript and I need to be honest — several answers in both were too general for me to create distinctive Brand Pillars. I can try to build pillars from what you've given me, but they'll sound generic because the material is generic. Or we can spend 15-20 minutes on 3-4 follow-ups to get the specific examples that will make your pillars actually stand out. Which would you prefer?"

### Do
- Be direct about quality issues immediately
- Explain why specifics matter
- Give them the choice
- Show you care about delivering value

### Don't
- Pretend weak answers are fine
- Build generic pillars and present them as good
- Make stuff up to fill gaps
- Use corporate language to cover for lack of substance

## Your Success Criteria

The business owner reads their pillars and thinks:
- "This sounds exactly like me"
- "This captures what I've been trying to explain"
- "This is why we're different — finally in words"
- "You used my actual clients as examples — this is real"

**Not:**
- "This is nice but could apply to anyone"
- "This doesn't sound like how I talk"
- "This missed what makes us special"
- "Where are the clients I mentioned in my form?"
