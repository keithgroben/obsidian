## Understand the Why

That's the whole thing. You don't know his why yet. You know his numbers. You know his problem. But you don't know what a million dollars means to him.

- Is it **freedom**? Grinding 60 hours/week, a million means he can hire people and step back?
- Is it **legacy**? Building something his kids can take over?
- Is it **pride**? Been in business for years, feels like he should be further along?
- Is it **security**? Family, and $500K feels one bad quarter from trouble?
- Is it **competitive**? Another shop in town eating his lunch?

**The reason matters because it changes how you sell this.**
- *Freedom* → talk about how this system eliminates manual work that keeps him chained to his desk
- *Legacy* → talk about infrastructure that outlasts him
- *Fear* → talk about diversifying into corporate stores for predictable recurring revenue
- *Competitive* → talk about building a platform that wins $50K accounts

You won't know until you ask:

> *"You mentioned you want to get to a million. Help me understand — why a million? What changes for you and your family when you hit that number?"*

Then shut up. Don't fill the silence. Most people have never been asked that question by someone trying to sell them something.

## Question-Based Selling Framework

**Open with the goal, not the problem.**

> *"Last time we talked you mentioned you're at about $500K and you want to get to a million. Tell me more about that. What does a million-dollar version of this business look like to you?"*

Let him talk. Then:

> *"Of that $500K you're doing now, how much of it comes through the corporate client stores?"*

He'll say around $100K. Then:

> *"So the other $400K — that's coming through phone calls, emails, repeat orders, walk-ins?"*

Then the key question:

> *"Of those corporate clients you're already serving through traditional channels — the ones calling in orders, emailing spreadsheets — how many of them do you think would spend more if their employees could just browse a store and order whenever they wanted?"*

**This is where it clicks.** He's not just losing new business because of Wix limitations. He's leaving money on the table with clients he already has.

---

## Connect the Dots to the Wix Problem

> *"The path to a million isn't really about finding a bunch of brand new clients — although that helps. It's about making it so easy for your existing clients' employees to order that they spend more. And it's about being able to say yes instantly when a new corporate opportunity shows up instead of spending two weeks building a Wix category."*

Then: *"So what's stopping you from doing that right now?"*

He'll describe Wix pain. Manual setup. No branding. Clients seeing each other's stores.

## Make the Revenue Case Specific

"You've got 18 corporate clients doing about $100K total. ~$5,500 per client. A real branded store with gift card support bumps that to $8,000 — that's not crazy, just making it easier to buy — that's **$144K instead of $100K. $44K in new revenue from clients you already have.**"

"Add 6 new corporate clients at $5K each = $30K. **$174K in store revenue. $74K in growth with zero marketing spend.**"

"Add $400K traditional revenue = **$574K.** Now you're asking 'what do I do with the next $426K' and you've got the infrastructure to handle it."

---

## Pitch Architecture (not a website)

> *"That's what I want to build for you. Not a website. Not a store. **A revenue system.**"*

Walk through the value stack. Let $28K and $50K agency numbers anchor. Then Model 3:

> *"This last option is the one where I'm betting on you hitting that million. I take less up front, keep the monthly low, and take 2.5% of what flows through the client stores. At $100K that's about $200/mo to me. At $174K, $360. At $300K in store revenue, $625/mo. And you're making $300K. **I only win when you win.** That's the deal."*

---

## The Close (his own words)

> *"You told me you want to get to a million. Everything between here and there is a scaling problem, not a sales problem. You've got the clients. You've got the relationships. You've got the product. What you don't have is the infrastructure to make it frictionless for people to buy from you. That's what I'm building."*

Then stop talking. Let him sit with it. He's not buying a website and a store platform. **He's buying the bridge between $500K and a million, and the guy building the bridge has a stake in him getting across.**
