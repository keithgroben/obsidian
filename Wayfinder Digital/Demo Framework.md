# Demo Framework

*Source: Notion — https://www.notion.so/3056abe00ba481908489dde6394fc6fc*

This is your strict format for every presentation, demo, or walkthrough you build. **One pain, one system, one invitation. No exceptions.**

---

## The Build Checklist
Answer these five questions in one sentence each before you create any demo. If you can't, the demo isn't ready.

1. What pain does this person fall asleep thinking about?
2. What do I already know about *this specific person's* version of that pain? *(Funnel version: What's the most common specific version of this pain in my target audience?)*
3. What system have I built that eliminates or reduces it?
4. What will I show on screen that makes this undeniable?
5. What is the single next step I'm inviting them into?

---

## Slide Structure (5 slides total, bookends around live demo)

1. **Title Slide** — Initiative name, your name, one-line value thesis
2. **Pain Slide** — Pain statement in their language. One sentence that hits.
3. **"Let Me Show You" Transition** — Minimal. Signals shift from talking to showing.
4. **"What This Means For You" Bridge** — After the demo. Bridges what they saw to their situation.
5. **Invitation Slide** — One next step. One way to take it.

The demo lives between slides 3 and 4. That's the substance. **The slides are the frame, not the painting.**

---

## Section 1: "You Know This Feeling" — 2 minutes max
State the pain in their language. Not your framework language. The words they'd text a friend. One pain statement + one brief story. More than 2 minutes = performing instead of connecting.

## Section 2: "Watch This" — Demo dictates length
Live demonstration. Screen share. Walkthrough. No slides explaining what it does — **the thing itself doing what it does.** You narrate what they're seeing, but the system is the star.

**The rule: if you can show it, don't say it.** Every claim should be visible on screen.

**Show what works, name what's coming, never mock up what doesn't exist.** A half-built system shown honestly is more compelling than a fully designed mockup.

## Section 3: "Now Imagine This Is Yours" — 2-3 minutes
Connect what they just saw to their specific situation. Reference a bottleneck, frustration, or goal they told you about.

## Section 4: "Here's the Door" — 1 minute
One clear next step. Not three options. Not a package breakdown. One invitation.

---

## The Rules
1. **The demo dictates the length.** Don't pad it.
2. **No more than 5 slides before the live demo.** Ideally 2-3.
3. **Show what works, name what's coming, never mock up what doesn't exist.**
4. **One pain, one system, one invitation.** Two systems = two demos.
5. **Funnel version and live version use the same core asset.** One recording, many contexts.

---

## Atomization Path
Once recorded, the demo becomes pillar content:
- YouTube Shorts — strongest 60-second moments
- LinkedIn post — core pain statement + insight from Section 1
- Substack piece — deeper dive on the framework
- Email — build checklist as personal note
- Graphic post — one-liner version of pain + invitation

**The demo is the source. Everything else is a fragment of it.**
