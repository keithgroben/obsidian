## YOUR BUSINESS MAKES TWO TYPES OF DECISIONS

| OBJECTIVE DECISIONS | SUBJECTIVE DECISIONS | WHY THIS MATTERS |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes or no. One of 3–5 choices.** | **Depends on variables. Requires judgment.** | **Objective → automated.** |
| Definable, trainable, seldom change | "Hey boss, what do we do here?" | **Subjective → assisted.** |
| Routine you've learned and taught your staff | Simple ones and complex ones | AI doesn't do wisdom. Probably never will. |
| *Example: Customer calls for quote. Staff looks up product, checks pricing, applies margin, sends it. AI can do that right now.* | *Example: Same customer calls upset. Do you discount? Redo? Hold firm? Needs judgment, relationship awareness, customer history.* | But AI can pull up history and hand it to your employee so they decide better, faster. |

You know objective decisions exist in your business because switching accounting software or CRM is painful. The pain is the encoded processes.

## MAKING YOUR BUSINESS TALK COMPUTER

AI can't listen to you describe your day and automatically create a system. **You sit down and map your processes end to end.**

Take a sheet of paper and a pencil. Write down every process: Marketing. Lead capture. Talking to leads. Converting. Following up. Scheduling. Sales call. Getting the quote out. Closing. Starting the work. Communicating between departments. HR. Payroll. Finance.

Now pick one. Map every step. Draw a flowchart. You'll see:
- Objective steps — **definable, repeatable, automatable**
- Subjective steps — **judgment calls that need a person**

Once you see that, you know exactly where AI fits. **In the specific places where it multiplies what your people can do.**

**Unless your business can be described in structured terms — with inputs, outputs, and clear status changes — you will never be able to build an intelligence layer on top of it.**
