# The Value Delivery Loop

*Source: Notion — https://www.notion.so/3106abe00ba4816fbb3fea243910d589*

## The Universal Cycle
Every initiative across every domain follows the same loop. A software product, a marketing campaign, a book, a video series. They all share this structure.

### Phase 1: Origin (Strategy)
Where ambiguity lives by design. Moving from fuzzy to focused.
Value Delivery Plan (what outcome for whom?) → UVP (why you/this approach?) → MVP (what's the minimum thing we ship to test the thesis?)

### Phase 2: Content Creation (Production)
What type of deliverable is being produced, and what expertise is required. The workflows and friction reduction (automation layer) sit beneath this. The tracker only needs to see: type, expert(s), and status.

### Phase 3: Publish (Distribution)
The moment of deployment. Track not just "did it go out" but where and to whom. Channel, audience segment, and date.

### Phase 4: Monitor (Measurement)
Two categories: **digital signals** (analytics, platform metrics, open rates, impressions) and **human signals** (meeting feedback, verbal responses, relationship shifts). The human signals are the ones most people drop.

### Phase 5: Result (Evaluation)
Monitor is raw signal. Result is judgment: did we get what we wanted? Did we get things we didn't want? How far off were we?

## The Feedback Loop

The result feeds back to one of three decisions:

- **Iterate** — Signal says we're on to something but execution needs adjustment. Change content, channel, messaging, format.
- **Evolve** — Signal says the underlying thesis needs refinement. Back to Origin. Sharpen UVP, redefine MVP, maybe shift audience.
- **End** — Signal says this isn't working, further investment isn't justified. Kill it gracefully and reclaim the energy.

The third is the hardest and the most valuable. **If this system makes it easier to end things with confidence rather than guilt, it's worth its weight in gold.**

## The Cadence
Feedback loop cadence is tied to the initiative's natural rhythm, not an arbitrary schedule. A software product might get a monthly review. A piece of copy might complete its entire loop in a day. Forcing them into the same cadence is over-engineering.
