# The Buried Operator ICP

*Source: Notion — https://www.notion.so/3106abe00ba48198a4aae849285e6e4e*

## Ideal Customer Profile: The Buried Operator

### Who they are
Owner-operator of a $500K-$5M business. Typically trades or service business. 3-15 employees. Built the business on their own expertise and they're good at what they do. **That's the problem.**

### What's true about them
Everything runs through their head. They are sales team, quality control, estimator, trainer, customer relationship manager, and production bottleneck all at once. No documented processes because the process is them. Tried hiring but it didn't stick — couldn't transfer what they know fast enough. Looked at software but nothing fits how their shop actually works, and they don't have time to configure it.

### What they feel
**Trapped. Not failing.** That's the cruel part. The business makes money. But they can't step away for a day without something breaking. First one in, last one out — not because they want to be, but because nobody else can make the decisions they make. Guilty about missing family time. Frustrated that the business they built to create freedom has become the thing that took it away.

### What they've tried
- Hired admin/office manager — helps but doesn't solve the core problem
- Bought CRM software that's half-populated
- Marketing seminars that left them with a binder they never opened
- Maybe a marketing agency once — felt like they burned money
- Skeptical of consultants because the last one gave them a PDF of recommendations they didn't have time to implement

### What they believe that keeps them stuck
**"Nobody can do it the way I do it."** Simultaneously true and the belief that's killing them. They believe systematizing means losing the personal touch. They think automation is for big companies with IT departments. They think they need to hire more people when what they actually need is to capture what's in their head and let a system run it.

### What they actually need
Someone who can extract the knowledge trapped in their head, turn it into systems that run without them, and do it without requiring them to stop working for six months while it gets built. **They need to see it working before they believe it. They need proof, not promises.**

### Where they are
Not on LinkedIn thinking about "operational architecture." They're in Facebook groups for their trade. At industry events and local business meetups. Talking to other shop owners who have the same problems. Word of mouth and referrals, not marketing funnels. If they see your content, it'll be because someone they trust shared it or because you showed up where they already hang out.

### What opens the door
**A demo. Not a pitch deck. Not a white paper.** A working system that solves a problem they recognize, shown to them by someone who clearly understands their world. The moment they see their bottleneck being solved on a screen, the skepticism breaks. **They don't need to be convinced. They need to be shown.**

### What they'll pay for
**Results, not hours.** They don't want a retainer. They've been burned by retainers. They want to see the system, understand what it does, and pay for the outcome. "Build this for my shop and I'll pay you when it works" is closer to how they think. **The RaaS model fits this perfectly.**
