# It's Not an Information Problem — It's a Formation Problem

## The core truth
- They see the right answer. The info is sound. They still don't move.
- Why: they were *formed* to believe deciding alone is the sin.
- You can't fix a formation problem with better information.

## What they call "honor" is usually fear in virtue's clothing
- Honor = the permission slip to avoid a hard conversation.
- $8K for 25 people on stage = paying to not tell 24 they're not needed.
- Hidden side-decision-maker = director protecting himself from owning it alone.
- Fasting about headcount 2.5 months out = avoiding a decision by routing it through a process no one can be blamed for.

## The real engine: accountability-avoidance, sanctified
- Authority means someone can be blamed, can fail visibly.
- Diffusion means no one ever has to.
- Christian culture hands them holy words for it: honor, humility, unity, waiting on the Lord.
- It's stickier here not because Christians are worse — because the *vocabulary* is better.

## Key terms (the mechanisms)
- **Status by proximity** — identity from being *near* the big thing, not doing it.
- **Bikeshedding** — loud energy on trivial parts, silence on the hard ones.
- **Social loafing** — big group = no single owner = nothing moves.
- **Sunk identity** — rejecting help because accepting it exposes the gap.
- **Committee-as-deliverable** — the meeting *feels* like the work.

## Why help bounces off
- You can't help a group out of a problem it's structured to maintain.
- The committee isn't broken on the way to its goal — for most, the committee *is* the goal.
- Better plan = a trade they don't want: efficiency for belonging.
- MercyMe proves it: they refused the whole outcome because saying yes concentrates the wheel.

## The bigger picture (the sharpest point)
- The church spent decades dismantling single-point authority — sold as virtue.
- Built a system where no one can abuse authority because no one *has* any.
- No risk of a bad king = certainty of no king. A field with no owner never gets planted.
- This isn't the biblical model — it's the *religious* one. Jesus concentrated, sent, delegated real authority, let people own outcomes.

## The diagnostic — outcome group vs. feeling group
- One question: is this group built to produce an **outcome** or a **feeling**?
- Outcome group → decisions concentrate toward those who can execute → help lands.
- Feeling group → decisions diffuse so everyone stays comfortable → help bounces, every time.
- You can tell in about one meeting.

## Finding new wineskins
- Watch for who is **willing to be blamed.**
- They say "I'll own this — if it fails, it's on me."
- They *pray and then decide* — not pray *instead of* deciding.
- They reduce the number of people who touch a decision, not increase it.
- They hear "you weren't needed there" without it wounding their identity.
- They often carry scar tissue — called proud for doing the thing that worked.

## How to help the right way (the lesson)
- Don't try to convert the old wineskin — it's transformation, not argument.
- Structure before strategy. Negotiate the structure *first*, before touching the work.
- Small empowered build team + bounded ceremonial role for everyone else.
- If they won't change the structure, the strategy was never going to take.
- Don't fix the feeling group. Decline, or find the few already transformed, and pour there.
