# 006 - The Most Important Thing You Can Do for Prospecting Success
## Summary
Hosts John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot tackle "the one thing" question from Gary Keller's book: what single activity makes everything else easier or unnecessary for prospecting? Ted argues it is outreach via phone calls. Using a digital-marketing cost comparison, they show an hour of calling yields more permission-based leads at a fraction of pay-per-click cost. Calls are rare, personal, and let you collect qualifying information. They then explain why calling feels hard, reframing rejection as impersonal (product, timing, or methodology) and showing how routines, time blocking, and prepared systems conserve daily energy so prospecting becomes effortless.
## Key Takeaways
- Apply the 'one thing' filter to prospecting: identify the activity that makes everything else easier or unnecessary, which the hosts argue is calling-based outreach tied to your [[Call Objective]].
- Phone calls beat expensive digital ads on return; an hour of calls can produce 5-10 permission emails for the cost of your time, versus thousands of dollars in pay-per-click for the same result.
- Calls are rare and valuable because most salespeople avoid them and avoid genuine [[Follow-Up]], leaving a low-competition, wide-open landscape.
- Person-to-person contact lets you gather Qualifying Information and the customer's real pain points, which a landing page or [[Mailing Database]] alone can never reveal.
- Rejection is almost never personal; people reject the product (including price), the timing, or the methodology, so a no is information, not an insult, as covered in [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Rejection only turns personal when you ignore a legitimate objection and hover over the target instead of respecting the person's answer.
- Protect a daily uninterrupted block using [[Time Blocking]] so calls get done first, before spreadsheets and internal work drain your energy.
- Build turnkey systems, a pre-sorted list with an [[Ascending Sort]] in a [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]], pre-written collateral, and a ready 6.6 The One-Pager so outreach runs on autopilot like a track to run on.
