# 014 - How to Take the Pain Out of Prospecting
## Summary
John Stevenson and Ted Stevenot tackle call reluctance, reframing it as a universal "action reluctance" rooted in perceived pain. Drawing on Daniel Kahneman's System 1 and System 2 thinking, they argue the emotional, fast-reacting mind floods you with anticipated pain before a call, while skipping calls produces a worse second pain: regret. The cure is to break prospecting into small steps that "shrink the job to size," build belief that the system works, prepare a menu of responses, and set well-designed expectations so that data and relationships, not just sales, count as a good day. Routines lower willpower burn until calling becomes effortless.
## Key Takeaways
- Call reluctance shows up as two pains: avoidance pain before the call and regret pain after skipping it, and the regret pain is usually the more damaging one to long-term success.
- Borrowing Kahneman's framework, the fast emotional 'System 1' mind floods you with perceived pain so you must calm it and align it with the rational plan, which connects directly to [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Breaking prospecting into small concrete steps shrinks an intimidating job down to size, defeating the irrational mind's tendency to wrap itself around a whole mountain.
- Believing your system works produces real momentum, since success is the progressive achievement of a worthy goal, not just the final result, which mirrors the case made in [[The Importance of Prospecting]].
- Before calling you need the right gear: a database, a list, a script, and protected calling time, which is why [[Time Blocking]] and a clean [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]] reduce the friction of starting.
- Carry a 'menu' of responses like a military forward observer so any prospect reply has a planned answer, leaning on [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers]] and content marketing to build bridges after the call.
- Set well-designed expectations so a day with no finished leads still counts as valuable because you collected qualifying data and added people to your [[Plus List]], which ties to [[4.1 Every Day of Phoning Is a Good Day]].
- Rejection usually targets timing, methodology, or the product itself rather than you personally, so separating your self-worth from the response is essential to sustaining the work.
