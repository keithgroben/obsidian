# 022 - Part 2 of 3 - Using PPC Strategies for Lead Generation
## Summary
Part two of a three-part digital strategy series, this episode covers pay-per-click (PPC) advertising as a second channel for getting eyes on content. John and Ted recap the AIDA model (awareness, interest, desire, action) from the SEO episode, then break PPC into five strategies: display, search, social, affiliate, and remarketing. They argue every dollar should map to a trackable conversion metric (dismissing "brand lift" as worthless for most businesses) and that A/B testing one variable at a time, competitive analysis, and single-keyword ad groups (SKAGs) compound results exponentially across the funnel.
## Key Takeaways
- PPC is an umbrella term for online advertising that breaks into five core strategies: display, search, social, affiliate, and remarketing, each tied to where a prospect sits in the funnel and reinforcing [[Lead Generation]].
- Every PPC dollar must map to a trackable conversion metric; the hosts dismiss 'brand lift' as worthless for any business whose product isn't sitting on a store shelf like Coke, a stance that echoes the discipline of [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]].
- Match the conversion metric to the AIDA stage: a cookie for interest, a verified email/phone return path for desire, and an actual value exchange (a sale) for action.
- A/B test only one variable at a time, testing a page against itself with a single small change, or you get 'lousy science' and can't tell what moved the needle.
- Competitive analysis reveals what rivals pay per click at each funnel stage and uncovers cheaper 'orthogonal' keywords (like a makeup brand bidding on ingredient names) to reach customers others overbid for, a way of [[2.1 Defining Your Market]] more cheaply.
- Single-keyword ad groups (SKAGs) signal hyper-relevance to Google, raising quality score and lowering cost-per-click by putting the keyword in the headline, display URL, and description.
- Keyword match types trade relevance for reach: exact match is most relevant but lowest volume, broad match modifier casts widest and can burn budget on irrelevant searches (like Pokemon clicks on a barcode-scanner ad).
- The whole digital system circles back on itself: paid results that convert should be folded into your organic SEO content, and applying the 80/20 rule means optimizing the 1-2 funnel pathways that drive most of your sales.
