# 045 - How to Not Fail with Prospecting
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot tackle the psychological side of prospecting: how not to fail. The core argument, anchored in Jim Rohn's two formulas, is that success comes from a few simple disciplines practiced every day, while failure comes from a few errors in judgment repeated daily. Ted warns against burning out through "massive action" and lays out a six-part daily routine (sleep/diet/exercise, time with people, value-increasing time blocks, reflection, and continuous learning). The episode argues prospecting is a marathon built on protected, repeatable routines you actually enjoy, not heroic one-day sprints.
## Key Takeaways
- Success in prospecting comes from a few simple disciplines practiced every day, while failure comes from a few errors in judgment repeated daily, which is why [[6.4 Persistence]] matters more than any single heroic effort.
- Beware of 'massive action' burnout: Ted's record 750-call day worked only because he prepared like a marathoner, and those days never actually built his career, so treat prospecting as a marathon not a sprint.
- Protect a dedicated calling window through [[Time Blocking]], setting an uninterrupted appointment with the phone every day so the most important work is sure to happen.
- Knowledge without action is a form of delusion (and procrastination); you must pair study and reflection with a routine to actually move forward.
- Build the habit by making the first steps easy, the way you would only walk five minutes at the gym at first, so the routine takes hold before you chase results.
- Align your day so your highest-value, hardest tasks ('eat that frog') land at your freshest energy, keeping the calling block in a protected inner vault.
- Reflect at day's end to celebrate hitting your routine, because the dopamine reward of completing the process is the glue that keeps the disciplines repeatable.
- The fear of failure usually stems from feeling unprepared and from unmanaged expectations, so when it comes to [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]] you build internal evidence of your own ability over time.
