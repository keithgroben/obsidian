# 029 - Cold Calling Tips, Scripts, and Objectives
## Summary
Hosts John and Ted open with Anders Ericsson's book "Peak," distinguishing purposeful from deliberate practice, then apply that lens to cold calling. Ted argues that calling is a craft you master over time by following proven models, pushing your comfort zone, tracking numbers, and extracting value from every outcome rather than chasing one result. They walk through chapter three of the Prospect Factory book: three call objectives, the five basic call outcomes with real percentages from a primary list, and voice tips covering pace, tone, temperament, and time blocking.
## Key Takeaways
- Cold calling is a discipline you improve through deliberate practice, not a one-shot gamble: track your numbers, push past your comfort zone, and follow proven models so you don't reinvent the wheel, which maps directly to [[3.3 General Overviews on Calling]].
- Always begin with a clear [[Call Objective]], but treat outcomes as a continuum (the 'lobster trap') rather than a single pass/fail result, so you can extract value from every interaction described in [[3.1 Call Objectives]].
- The three calling objectives are to put yourself comfortably on the field, open a door for current or future interest, and set a clear daily expectation of victory that can include disqualifying people, not just landing leads.
- Expect the five basic outcomes in predictable proportions on a [[Primary List]]: roughly 45% unavailable, 25% disqualified, 20% not interested, 8% future interest, and around 2% interested now, as laid out in [[3.2 The Five Basic Outcomes to Most Calls]].
- Better-quality and currently-interested prospects get separated into a [[Plus List]] so you can spend more time, money, and mailings on them.
- Use a [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]] to log follow-up dates, future-interest notes, and qualifying details so you call people back exactly when they're ready to look.
- Deliver a [[Call Voice]] that is clear, even-toned, and authoritative (ending sentences on a down tone) to achieve [[Professional Speech]] that sounds confident rather than salesy.
- Keep a brisk, metronome-like [[Call Pace]] and use [[Time Blocking]] to protect a disciplined calling routine, staying calm and non-confrontational so every call is safe for you and the prospect.
