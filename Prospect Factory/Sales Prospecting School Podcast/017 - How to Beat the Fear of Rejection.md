# 017 - How to Beat the Fear of Rejection
## Summary
Hosts John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot interview Dr. Loretta Breuning, author of "Habits of a Happy Brain," to explain why prospecting feels so painful and how to push through it. She breaks down the four "happy chemicals" (dopamine, oxytocin, serotonin, endorphin) plus cortisol, framing rejection as an ancient mammalian survival response rather than a personal verdict. The core argument: fear of rejection comes from inherited circuits wired early in life, but salespeople can rewire them through repetition, small rewarded steps, and reframing each prospect as one of many irons in the fire rather than the gatekeeper to their future.
## Key Takeaways
- Rejection rarely targets you personally; prospects usually reject the product, the timing, or the delivery method, which is why separating those reasons protects the salesperson's psyche when [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Dopamine is the 'I can get it' chemical of expectation and search, so building skill at extracting value from a call gives you more chances at a small win and keeps you motivated to keep Making Calls for Your Prospect Factory.
- Cortisol wires bad feelings faster than dopamine wires good ones, so an unprepared, painful outreach session can train call reluctance; preparation and a clear [[Call Objective]] reduce that risk.
- Setting up a track to run on (preloaded call lists, a CRM, and templated letters or emails) conserves mental energy so you stay in the game, which is the practical payoff of [[2.4 Call Cycles]] and a [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]].
- The hardest part is starting; just making the first call gets you over the threshold, much like Breuning's 15-minute garage rule for beating overwhelm.
- Rewiring a circuit takes repetition (roughly 45 days) of small steps plus an immediate reward, because the brain only builds a new pathway when you expect to feel good doing the work.
- You can have a great day on the phone without closing a single finished lead if you measure success by qualifying the list and earning permission to mail, which is the logic behind [[4.1 Every Day of Phoning Is a Good Day]].
- Treat each prospect as one of a hundred irons in the fire rather than the gatekeeper to your dream, so no single rejection has the power to block your path, and lean on [[6.4 Persistence]].
