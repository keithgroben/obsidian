# 036 - Automation for Lead Generation
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot break down marketing automation for lead generation: the systems that take over follow-up after a prospect raises a hand and gives a return path. They lay out seven keys to automating sales follow-up: headline, structure, copy, delivery systems, workflows, triggers, and woven-in feedback checkpoints. The core argument is that automation should start with the sales message and copy structure, not the technology. Build a cohesive, themed cascade tied to the prospect's original interest, mix old-school and new-school delivery, template it for reuse, and weave in short check-in messages so the system stays relationship-driven rather than spammy.
## Key Takeaways
- Automation begins with the message, not the tool: nail the headline and copy structure first or you will be stuck inventing meaningless messages later, which is the same discipline behind [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers]].
- The whole cascade fires off the moment a prospect gives a return path, so the system must be built to handle individualized [[5.2 Automation and Follow Up]] at scale without burying you.
- Keep every message tied to the theme the prospect met you on; selling unrelated or deeply discounted products signals desperity and torches credibility, a mindset point related to [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]] and [[6.2 Keeping the Information You Collect to Yourself]].
- Mix old-school and new-school delivery: physical mail, a Dan Kennedy shock-and-awe package, or a phone call woven into an email sequence creates impact the inbox alone cannot, echoing [[4.2 Synergy Between the Plus List and Direct Mail]].
- Define workflows by what, when, and how, then connect them with triggers that act as checkpoints with both an input value (information you feed the system) and an output value (the next action the system takes).
- Template your automation so it gives expected, replicable outcomes you can swap between products instead of rebuilding from scratch every time, the same logic that powers a good [[Call-Cycle]].
- Weave short, personal feedback messages ("any questions?", "how did I do?") into the sequence and route replies straight to the salesperson so the system surfaces real conversations and follow-up opportunities like [[5.1 Tracking Follow-Up with Status Codes]].
- Use a CRM or email service provider with real workflow tools (they name Zoho, MailChimp, Salesforce, Infusionsoft) and let lead scoring escalate a contact through [[Minimum Qualification Standard]] from cold to on-fire prospect.
