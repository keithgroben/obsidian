# 035 - 25 Call Outcomes and How to Handle Them
## Summary
Ted Stevenot walks through roughly 25 common responses prospects give on B2B sales calls and how he handles each. The core argument: stop trying to overcome objections. Instead, "translate English into English" — decode what a prospect's words actually mean (usually "not interested currently," "disqualified," or "don't call me"), assign it a numerical code in your CRM, respond respectfully, and move on. Most varied responses collapse into a handful of outcomes. By categorizing and recording every call, you extract value, stay forgettable rather than memorable-in-a-bad-way, and protect your mindset against rejection.
## Key Takeaways
- The whole method rests on a three-step process: hear what the prospect says, translate it into an underlying meaning, then assign it a numerical code so the call becomes queryable later, which is the heart of [[3.2 The Five Basic Outcomes to Most Calls]].
- Each outcome maps to an [[OK Code]] entered in the [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]]: not-interested is a 1, mail-permission is a 2, not-available is a 3, a finished lead is a 6, do-not-call is an 8, and disqualified is a 9.
- Never overcome objections or get drawn into clarifying when a prospect gives an illogical reason; the temptation to explain your differentiation almost always blows up the call, so just respect the no and move on.
- Distinguish a prospect who is merely not-interested-now from one who is genuinely disqualified (OK Code 9) — disqualified prospects get sequestered on a master list and matched out of future [[Call-Cycle]] runs so you never call them again.
- When the person you want isn't available, pivot to asking permission to mail something rather than chasing a callback, turning a gatekeeper win into your win and feeding your [[Plus List]] via direct mail.
- Hostile, patronizing, and belligerent responses ('we're with Ajax,' 'you're wasting your time,' 'why?') are usually just defensive ways of saying no from people who can't comfortably refuse — give them an easy out like 'sounds like you're happy with your current provider.'
- Stay forgettable: winning a verbal fight loses the war, because a prospect you humiliate remembers you forever and will never buy, a key lesson in [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Don't try to close the whole sale in one call; use the phone to qualify and build a return path, then drip follow-up materials and a [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]] mindset that you must collect no's to get yes's.
