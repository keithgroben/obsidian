# 021 - Part 1 of 3 - How to Craft a Digital Strategy for Lead Generation
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot open a three-part series on building a digital lead-generation strategy, framing everything around the AIDA model (Awareness, Interest, Desire, Action) and arguing that branding only works inside a funnel that captures a return path. Part 1 focuses on Awareness through SEO, organic search, and content marketing. They define SEO vs. SEM, give three quick-start hacks (WordPress + Yoast + Google Search Console/Analytics), explain hub and long-tail keywords, cornerstone "start here" pages, gating content for value exchange, sourcing keywords from sales/support/tools, building a promotion schedule, owning your own hub website, and atomizing content across syndicated channels.
## Key Takeaways
- The hosts overlay digital marketing onto the AIDA model (Awareness, Interest, Desire, Action) and argue branding only pays off when woven into a funnel with a return path, supporting their broader emphasis on [[Lead Generation]].
- Awareness is digital obscurity-removal done through content, advertising, and partnerships, the same goal phone calls achieve in the old-school approach to [[Lead Generation]].
- Three SEO quick-start hacks: build on WordPress with proper sitemaps, install the Yoast SEO plugin, and set up Google Search Console plus Analytics and Tag Manager so you can submit your sitemap and get re-indexed on demand.
- Skip mission statements and about pages as primary content; build the site around a hub keyword and a cornerstone 'start here' page that branches into long-tail articles answering the questions people actually search.
- Gate content by funnel stage: give awareness-level material away freely, but require a value exchange (like an email) for interest and desire content so you capture a return path and learn about the customer.
- Source keywords and content from four places: salespeople, support staff, advertising tools like Google Keyword Planner and SEMrush, and internal brainstorming, then reconcile your vocabulary with how the market actually talks.
- Run a consistent promotion schedule that takes one problem or topic and hits it across sales, support, and marketing over weeks rather than randomly demanding articles, all aligned to [[Digital Marketing & Content]].
- Own your hub (your website) for control and longevity, then atomize one piece of content and syndicate it across channels like SoundCloud-to-iTunes rather than spreading thin across every platform at once.
