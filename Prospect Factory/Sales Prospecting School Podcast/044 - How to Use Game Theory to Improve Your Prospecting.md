# 044 - How to Use Game Theory to Improve Your Prospecting
## Summary
John and Ted Stevenot use "push forward combat," a game-design mechanic from the video game Doom, as a lens for prospecting. Its four tenets map onto sales: speed of the player (keep calling, never go inactive), individuality of the opponent (learn each prospect's "tells" by listening), distinctiveness of tools (build varied follow-up materials), and perceived power of the player (confidence plus qualification). The core argument is that momentum, deliberate practice, and reframing calls as a continuous, winnable game beat rejection and resistance. They close by warning against retreating, hiding behind passive marketing, and sniping from a distance.
## Key Takeaways
- Treat every block of phoning as forward motion rather than a hunt for instant sales; like a video game where standing still gets you killed, inactivity lets rejection and doubt build, so [[4.1 Every Day of Phoning Is a Good Day]] is the right mental frame.
- Set the right [[Call Objective]] before you dial: a day spent qualifying with no closed sale can still be a great day because gathering even three pieces of Qualifying Information from a list of ten creates real value.
- Learn each prospect's 'tell' the way a gamer learns an enemy's attack pattern; after enough calls the common responses become predictable, which is exactly what [[3.2 The Five Basic Outcomes to Most Calls]] captures.
- Codify every interaction with an [[OK Code]] so calling becomes deliberate practice instead of meaningless conversations you never learn from.
- Use the 'flashcard' approach with simple binary gates rather than dumping six questions at once, then graduate the prospect to a qualified [[Plus List]] for ongoing mail and follow-up.
- Build distinctive, varied tools and materials geared to the audience so you are never walking into a 'boss fight' with the wrong loadout; sales letters, drip sequences, and stage-specific guides are part of [[5.2 Automation and Follow Up]].
- The 'power of the player' is confidence built on volume plus the discipline of not taking rejection personally, which is the heart of [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Watch for the three retreat behaviors that signal you have left the fight: bailing out, hiding behind passive advertising, and sniping from a distance via social media instead of making direct calls.
