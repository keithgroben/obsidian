# 007 - How to Fill Your Sales Pipeline
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot explain the sales pipeline as a system that produces both immediate "finished leads" and future leads seeded weeks or months earlier through consistent, proactive outreach. They walk through four channels for filling the pipeline: phone calls, email, postal mail, and digital (organic and paid). The core argument is that no single day of outreach must produce a sale to be a good day, because qualifying people, sending materials, and building relationships all move leads forward over roughly a three-month ramp. They stress permission-based contact, return paths over vanity metrics, owning channels you control, and time blocking to keep outreach habitual.
## Key Takeaways
- A sales pipeline serves two purposes at once: securing today's finished lead and seeding prospects who will raise their hand weeks or months later, so judge a day by activity moved forward, not only by sales closed (see [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]]).
- There are four channels for filling the pipeline (calls, email, mail, digital), and every outreach must include a proactive invitation so prospects can easily reconnect when they are ready, tying directly to the [[Plus List]] of people who asked for something.
- Phone calls are the richest channel because conversation lets you collect Qualifying Information and uncover needs the prospect did not originally call about, often revealing a different product fit (see [[3.1 Call Objectives]]).
- Mail wins attention precisely because so few people send it now, and a strong gatekeeper is a reason to mail a 6.6 The One-Pager-style piece rather than a reason to give up.
- Digital outreach is only valuable when it produces a return path (an email subscriber, a contact) or a sale; brand awareness and vanity metrics like likes and views are dangerous, expensive distractions ([[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]]).
- Favor channels you control: a website beats a Facebook page or YouTube channel whose reach or monetization the platform can switch off at any time.
- Recurring questions from calls and mail should become reusable content so you can answer with a ready link, giving you leverage and deeper relationship-building (see [[5.2 Automation and Follow Up]]).
- Use [[Time Blocking]] to protect distraction-free slots for calling, mailing, and content creation, because the system only works through consistent outreach over a roughly three-month ramp ([[6.4 Persistence]]).
