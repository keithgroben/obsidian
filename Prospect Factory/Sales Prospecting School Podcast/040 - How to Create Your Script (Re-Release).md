# 040 - How to Create Your Script (Re-Release)
## Summary
Hosts John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot rerelease their most popular early episode, a deep dive on building and using a cold-calling script. Ted argues that effective prospecting starts before the call: a call-ready, compliant list, a cleared "runway" of distraction-free blocked time, and a steady call-to-call rhythm. His script is a tight "flashcard" (name, company, purpose) that you flash, then listen. The core philosophy is zero-resistance, accept-don't-argue selling: rejection is about product, timing, or method, never you personally, so you get in, get the code, and get out without hovering over the target.
## Key Takeaways
- Before dialing, you need a [[2.2 Putting Together Your Prospect List]] that is so clean it is mail-ready, plus [[Appendix A: Telemarketing Legislation]] compliance with do-not-call rules for your industry.
- Protect the activity with [[Time Blocking]]: clear the runway of all distractions because human focus works like an oven, not a microwave, and reopening the door bleeds out the heat.
- Ted's script is a concise 'flashcard' that delivers your name, company, and a single [[Call Objective]] to speak with the person in charge, then stops and waits for any response.
- Listen to what callers say and convert each response into an [[OK Code]] in your [[OK Field]] rather than getting lost in conversational details.
- All rejection breaks into three buckets, products, timing, or methods, and none of it is personal, which is the heart of [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Practice zero-resistance selling: ingress and egress fast, do not hover over the target, because pushing past a 'no' is what turns it into personal rejection and a take-me-off-your-list request.
- When you cannot reach the decision-maker, use the fallback 'would it be okay if I send something,' which both respects the gatekeeper and generates the permission behind the [[Plus List]] and the One-Pager.
- Follow up like Jim Rohn taught, talk to lots of people, be real nice, and take good notes, turning favorable calls into a long-term fan base via [[5.3 Follow-Up Letters]].
