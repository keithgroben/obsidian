# 032 - Lead Generation Strategies - The Four Horsemen
## Summary
John and Ted Stephenson lay out their "Four Horsemen" of lead generation: cornerstone content, a free guide/offer, a sales letter, and a follow-up funnel. Each piece links to the others so you never reach a prospect without a return path to reconnect. They argue cornerstone content earns SEO traffic, the free guide builds know-like-and-trust authority, the sales letter (or its digital twin, the landing page) is the most proactive lead driver, and the follow-up funnel converts and sustains the relationship. They stress writing it yourself, evolving a white paper into a book, building one product silo as a reusable template, and distinguishing a finite sales follow-up from an ongoing relationship list.
## Key Takeaways
- The core principle is that you should never reach out to anyone without a built-in return path to reconnect, turning every touchpoint into a relationship engine rather than a one-off interaction like handing over a business card.
- Cornerstone content is an SEO-optimized product page (roughly 1,000-2,000 words with header tags, keyword usage, and alt text) whose job is to rank and capture searchers, then leak them toward a free offer above the fold.
- The free guide can start as a three-page white paper and evolve over time into a full e-book or physical book (via CreateSpace), building authority and the know-like-and-trust relationship that drives buying decisions.
- The sales letter is the most dynamic of the Four Horsemen because it is proactive: you can mail it, hand it out door to door, or drive PPC/AdWords traffic to it, and it invites prospects into your system rather than waiting on search.
- A landing page (or squeeze page) is essentially the digital version of a sales letter, stripped of menus and distractions so the only paths are leaving or giving an email, and online versions should ask for the response sooner (above the fold).
- Split your follow-up into two tracks: a finite sales follow-up funnel with a clearly stated end (a deadline on the offer) and a separate long-term relationship email list that delivers ongoing value, so prospects never feel badgered across multiple lists.
- Tangible 'shock and awe' follow-up like a first-class autographed book, a mailed article, or Gary Halbert's dollar-bill letter stands out precisely because almost nobody sends physical, thoughtful touches anymore.
- Building all four pieces for one product creates a reusable template, since roughly 85% of a sales letter and funnel carries over to the next product silo with only the customized attention-getting opening needing a rewrite.
