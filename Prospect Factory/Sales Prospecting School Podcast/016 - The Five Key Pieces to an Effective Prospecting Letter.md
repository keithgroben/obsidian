# 016 - The Five Key Pieces to an Effective Prospecting Letter
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot break down the prospecting letter, framing it around one question: what is your goal? They lay out a continuum from direct sale, to generating current interest, to relationship/branding, arguing that capturing current interest with a clear reply path is the most valuable outcome. Ted traces how heavy phone prospecting and the early fax machine forced him into a one-page sales letter, then teaches its five fixed parts: a flag line, support, a general description with your value proposition, a reply prompt, and contact information. They stress brevity, system-one/system-two psychology, and building a stable of swappable one-pagers.
## Key Takeaways
- Every prospecting letter should start with a defined goal on a continuum from making a direct sale, to generating current interest, to relationship building or branding, and the hosts warn that branding without a measurable response is where you waste money.
- The highest-value outcome is current interest, which only counts when the prospect gives you a reply path; this maps directly to the [[Plus List]] and [[5.1 Tracking Follow-Up with Status Codes]] logic of capturing genuinely interested people.
- Long-form content can answer objections in advance for ready buyers, while short-form content connects with the emotional system-one reaction, and the right length always depends on the application.
- Ted's one-page letter grew out of being forced into a short format by fax-machine constraints, and it became the core of his [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers]] practice using permission-only outreach.
- Part one is the flag line or title, ideally a what or how question rather than a verb-led yes/no question, because open questions provoke introspection and carry the power dynamic of [[Professional Speech]].
- Part three, the general description, should lead with your why and a unique value proposition framed as how you guide the customer, never positioning yourself as the hero of the story.
- The reply prompt must be a concise, specific call to action with literal blanks to fill in, and asking for more information yields fewer but better-qualified leads, a tradeoff tied to the [[Minimum Qualification Standard]].
- Parts three, four, and five stay nearly identical across letters, so you build a stable of one-pagers and only swap the title and support, letting you run a [[Mail Cycle]] alongside a [[Call-Cycle]] with varied messaging.
