# 034 - 10 Rules for Marketing Your Business
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot distill Dan Kennedy's "No B.S. Direct Marketing" into ten (plus one) rules for marketing a business. The core argument: stop spending on brand building, likes, and pretty logos, and instead run direct-response marketing where every message asks the prospect to do something, captures a return path, and is held to measurable results. They cover making clear offers, giving reasons to respond now, writing precise instructions, setting accountable objectives, relentless follow-up, strong emotion-driven copy, mixing media channels, and testing your way to a champion message.
## Key Takeaways
- Every communication should ask the prospect to do something, ideally via a [[Call Objective]]-style action step, so you can keep score and let people raise their hand instead of just walking away.
- Always capture a return path (name, company, email, mailing address, phone) so you can keep the conversation going over time, which mirrors building a [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]] of people who have already shown interest.
- Give prospects a real reason to respond now using urgency, scarcity, exclusivity, or premium offers, the same emotional pull that closes a furniture-store 'sale ends Monday' deal.
- Write clear, specific instructions telling people exactly what to do, how to do it, and by when, removing every obstacle so the path acts almost like a [[Minimum Qualification Standard]] checklist anyone can follow.
- Set clear objectives and stay accountable to them by dropping mailings that fail and challenging the winners, judging each step (clicks, replies, sales) one stage of the funnel at a time.
- Do not pay for brand building, likes, or logo obsession; let the product people actually asked for become the brand, and build brand after customer acquisition, not before.
- There will always be follow-up: rescue stalled prospects, blend electronic outreach with offline touches like mailed letters and packages, and never drop the 90% of leads that most businesses abandon.
- Mix marketing channels for both safety and synergy, using phone outreach as a qualification tool to build a [[Plus List]]-style up-list that you then feed with direct mail and email.
- Keep a strict marketing diet focused on direct response where results are unambiguous, run small cheap tests, find a champion, and then try to beat it to restart the market again.
