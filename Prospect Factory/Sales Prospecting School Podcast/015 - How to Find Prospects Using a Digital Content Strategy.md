# 015 - How to Find Prospects Using a Digital Content Strategy
## Summary
Hosts John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot explore how to find prospects using a digital content strategy, blending old-school sales psychology with new-school online platforms. They argue that content is most powerful not as flashy promotion but in sales support and client education, and that businesses should pick one or two channels, build traction, and tie everything to concrete results. Using Simon Sinek's why/how/what framing as a funnel, they walk through setting goals, building an editorial calendar, and measuring outcomes. Their core message: chase a return path of permission-based contact, not vanity metrics, and treat digital as a marathon, not a sprint.
## Key Takeaways
- Content earns the most leverage in the fringe areas of sales support and client education, not in viral promotion, because helpful answers to rare questions become the top search result and inspire confidence in new prospects, which feeds directly into [[Lead Generation]].
- Pick a maximum of two or three channels and get a foothold in one first, because traction trumps everything; spreading across every platform that seems sexy just wastes time, energy, and budget without moving the cash register, a discipline that echoes [[2.1 Defining Your Market]].
- Match the content type to the goal: podcasts and video build personal know-like-and-trust relationships, while blogs and white papers build credibility and showcase accolades, so let the result you want dictate the format.
- Structure content as a funnel using Simon Sinek's why (the emotional story), how (the rational routes), and what (the action and follow-up), and run that full why/how/what cycle separately for each prospect persona, from obscure suspect to interested lead to loyal customer.
- Set goals, commit to a consistent editorial calendar (weekly is a sensible floor), and always anchor the strategy to results within a defined window so you can review what worked, drawing on [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]].
- The holy grail of conversion is a return path: a mutually beneficial moment where someone gives you permission to contact them again (Seth Godin's permission marketing), which beats vanity likes and shares that generate no path back.
- Make sure your KPIs actually indicate real value rather than dopamine-hit statistics, and benchmark against industry standards so you do not misjudge a strong 54 percent email open rate as a failure.
- Avoid chasing unicorns and the black hole of over-optimization (tweaking colors, fonts, and seconds of video); digital is a marathon that can take 18 months for serious traction, so start by telling your story in whatever format you are most comfortable with.
