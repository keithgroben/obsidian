# 025 - Listener Question - How to Prospect for a Niche Business
## Summary
Hosts John and Ted answer a listener question from Brian, who runs a Sarasota fishing-charter business, about growing a local niche business online. They argue any website platform (Wix or WordPress) can work if it meets four tests: control, ease of use, and compatibility with social networks and Google. They then lay out a local-SEO foundation: a home page, keyword-driven internal "cornerstone" pages supported by linked blog posts, Google My Business, and a footer map listing areas served. The core argument: dominate local search first, then turn traffic into value through phone contact and an email list built on a real value exchange.
## Key Takeaways
- Pick a website platform by four tests rather than brand loyalty: you must control and own the platform, find it easy to write and post on, and have it stay compatible with social networks and with Google's index, which connects directly to the work of [[2.1 Defining Your Market]].
- A platform you fully own is a foundation you can build on for years, whereas a presence built only on YouTube or Facebook can be cut in half overnight when their algorithm changes, so treat the owned website as the cornerstone of your [[Lead Generation]].
- Structure the site like a tent: the home page at the peak tells your story, then five or six internal cornerstone pages built around your major keywords hold up the walls, each supported by linked blog posts that prove topical expertise to Google.
- Claim and obsess over Google My Business, making it the style guide so your name, address, and hours match exactly across Facebook, your site, and Google, which is how you fight to land in the local three-business stack.
- Dominate local search first even for a globally competitive topic, because a small operator who owns the local SEO can surface to an out-of-state searcher looking for that specific destination, much like the focused targeting in [[2.1 Defining Your Market]].
- Turn content into value through two capture points: a phone setup that catches every vacation call, and an email list grown by trading a genuinely valuable free guide for the visitor's email, never with a bland 'join our list' prompt.
- Segment offerings by customer type (family vacation, corporate team-building, honeymoon, expert angler) as a way of defining who you serve, which mirrors building a usable [[Plus List]] of the people worth staying in touch with.
- Email is the biggest lever for a seasonal repeat business: collect addresses where you connect in person, send periodic image-rich updates and advance-booking offers, and you build the know-like-and-trust relationship that drives rebookings.
