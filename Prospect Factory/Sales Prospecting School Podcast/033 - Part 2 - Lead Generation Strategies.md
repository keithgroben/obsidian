# 033 - Part 2 - Lead Generation Strategies
## Summary
Part 2 of a two-part series on lead generation, hosts John and Ted unpack how to actually implement the "Four Horsemen" of digital lead gen: a sales letter, a lead magnet (free guide), a follow-up funnel, and cornerstone SEO content. Their core argument is that complexity kills execution, so you start with the smallest sustainable step. Using diet and exercise analogies, they argue the sales letter comes first because it is the most deployable, and that each piece feeds the next. They walk the problem-agitate-solution structure, headlines, and attention-grabbing tactics, and dismiss "start with why" in favor of action.
## Key Takeaways
- The hardest part of any marketing project is starting, so the move is to identify the smallest, most sustainable step and attack that first rather than the whole intimidating system, which supports the mindset work in [[6.4 Persistence]].
- Of the Four Horsemen, start with the sales letter because it is the most deployable and creates the soonest results, and it then becomes the workhorse content you email to anyone who asks for information.
- Build sales letters around the problem-agitate-solution structure, and David Frey's 12-step or Dan Kennedy's model so that each step interconnects and informs the next, much like the discipline of [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers]].
- Lead with the prospect's problem and deepest desires, not your product features, since 'my product doesn't mean anything unless it solves a problem' echoes the prospect-centered framing in [[3.1 Call Objectives]].
- Headlines grab attention but are easy to skip, so pair them with something tactile and unexpected (Gary Halbert's bag of dirt) to trigger curiosity and keep the reader engaged.
- The four pieces work as engine and gas: the letter, magnet, funnel, and SEO are the engine, while calling, mailing, emailing, and pay-per-click are the fuel that puts the message in front of eyes, reinforcing the synergy idea behind [[4.2 Synergy Between the Plus List and Direct Mail]].
- Once the sales letter is live you can run A/B testing on headlines and offers against a compiled list, learning your real response rates the way [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]] teaches.
- Digital tools like heat mapping and a follow-up funnel feed back downstream, telling you which three of six items prospects actually care about so you focus your letters and follow-up there, the same close-the-loop habit as [[5.2 Automation and Follow Up]].
