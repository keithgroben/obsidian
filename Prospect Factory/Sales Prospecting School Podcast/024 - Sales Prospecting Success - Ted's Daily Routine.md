# 024 - Sales Prospecting Success - Ted's Daily Routine
## Summary
John interviews Ted about the exact daily routine behind his prospecting success. Ted starts with a 5 a.m. mindset block — gym, paleo breakfast with his wife, journaling and naming the day's one essential task — then opens his CRM at 8 a.m. He sorts a filtered 20% list (about 7,000 records, 350 calls a week) by his last-call and OK fields, makes calls uninterrupted, fires template emails during calls, then prints envelopes and one-pagers for mail follow-up. The core argument: a pre-built, repeatable routine removes anxiety and makes the first call effortless.
## Key Takeaways
- Ted's edge came from abandoning forced-rejection selling for surfacing [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection|current interest]], which made prospecting painless and let his volume explode.
- He opens his CRM each morning and works a pre-saved, filtered subset of his database — about the top 20% — demonstrating disciplined [[2.6 Keeping Your Lists Updated|list maintenance]] and the value of a [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)|CRM]].
- Two fields drive everything: the [[Last Call Field]] sorted [[Ascending Sort|ascending]] surfaces uncalled blank records, while the [[OK Field]] codes every interaction outcome.
- He runs a defined [[Call-Cycle]], pacing roughly 7,000 records as 350 calls a week across a 20-week cycle so every prospect gets touched on schedule.
- Follow-up happens live: he drops [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers|one-pager]] email templates during or seconds after the call, then sorts by OK code afterward to decide who gets physical mail.
- He protects calling time fiercely — no inbound calls, auto-text replies, full focus — treating uninterrupted phone blocks like a non-negotiable appointment ([[4.1 Every Day of Phoning Is a Good Day]]).
- The most valuable list is the one he built himself over years; relationships, screening, and trust make it impossible for a competitor to simply copy ([[2.2 Putting Together Your Prospect List]]).
- Rejection is a zero issue after decades of reps; the bigger daily challenge is simply boredom, which the routine and genuine attention to each caller keep in check ([[6.4 Persistence]]).
