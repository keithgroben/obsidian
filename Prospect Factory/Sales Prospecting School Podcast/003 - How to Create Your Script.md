# 003 - How to Create Your Script
## Summary
This episode walks through how to build a telephone prospecting script and the prerequisites around it. Before dialing, you need a call-ready, compliant list, a blocked-off distraction-free time, and a defined call flow. The core technique is a tight "flash card" script (name, company, purpose) that you deliver and then go silent, listening to and coding the responses. The hosts argue rejection is never personal: people reject products, timing, or methods, so you accept the no, avoid hovering over the target, and move on. Interested prospects get follow-up and become finished leads.
## Key Takeaways
- Before you ever dial, you need a call-ready, compliant list scrubbed well enough to mail, since prospecting depends on clean data and respecting telemarketing rules (see [[2.2 Putting Together Your Prospect List]] and [[Appendix A: Telemarketing Legislation]]).
- Clear the runway with [[Time Blocking]]: block dedicated calling time, shut out interruptions, and treat task-switching as the enemy because humans build focus like an oven, not a microwave.
- The script is a concise 'flash card' that states your name, company, and purpose, then goes silent to let the prospect react however they want (see [[Call Voice]] and [[3.1 Call Objectives]]).
- As you call, listen and assign a numeric [[OK Code]] to each response so you categorize people systematically instead of getting lost in details (see [[2.5 OK Codes]]).
- Rejection breaks into three buckets, product, timing, and method, and none of them are personal, which is the key to handling [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Do not hover over the target: deliver the message, accept the no, and exit fast so rejection never escalates into personal hostility that makes you unforgettable for the wrong reasons.
- When you can't reach the decision-maker, fall back to asking 'would it be okay if I send something,' which protects the gatekeeper and opens the door to the [[Plus List]] and a One-Pager (see [[Part 6: The One-Pager]]).
- Keep all follow-up for after the call block so you preserve flow, and treat interested prospects as sparks to fan into long-term client relationships through being nice and taking good notes.
