# 008 - How to Maximize the Value of Your Prospecting
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot argue that prospecting is only worthwhile when it produces a "value conversion" — a reciprocal exchange that earns a return path back to the prospect. They define two core conversions: a "finished lead" (someone qualified enough to hear a proposal) and a digital subscriber (a name plus email). Brand building, social shares, likes, and comments are "shiny objects" that feel good but rarely convert. The hosts stress baking return paths into every outreach, tracking conversions with email providers and CRMs, setting measurable ratios, and treating prospecting as a long-cut process rather than a magic shortcut.
## Key Takeaways
- A value conversion is a one-to-one exchange where you give something useful and the prospect gives you a return path; everything in your outreach should be built to earn that path, which is the heart of [[The Importance of Prospecting]].
- A finished lead is someone interested enough to hear a proposal and who has given you enough Qualifying Information to build one, meeting your [[Minimum Qualification Standard]].
- An email subscriber (first name plus email) beats a thousand social followers or likes because email is a permission-based return path you control, not someone else's algorithm.
- Brand building, removing obscurity, and social proof are valuable only when paired with a mechanism to reconnect; without a funnel you can spend millions and never make a sale.
- Track conversions with measurable ratios so you know whether a day or week of calls succeeded — see [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]] and [[3.2 The Five Basic Outcomes to Most Calls]].
- A [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]] lets you extract layered value from calls — capturing names, [[OK Code]] qualification, and contacts you can later turn into a mailing list.
- Calls that earn permission to send mail feed direct-mail and [[The Plus List]] efforts, creating compounding follow-up value over time as covered in [[5.1 Tracking Follow-Up with Status Codes]].
- Beware retargeting and cookie tracking as a way to skip the human sales process; they work only as a follow-up assist inside a complete system that still has personal interaction and return paths.
