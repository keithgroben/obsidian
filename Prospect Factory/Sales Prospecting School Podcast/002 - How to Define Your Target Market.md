# 002 - How to Define Your Target Market
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot explain how to define your target market before any list building or outreach begins. They contrast the customer "avatar" or ideal prospect against a Minimum Qualification Standard, the smallest profitable customer who keeps the doors open while you chase bigger fish. The core argument: pursue traction with controllable, bread-and-butter prospects within a serviceable distance rather than fixating on viral growth or an unreachable perfect client. They then walk through finding lists (libraries, trade directories, cloud databases), scrubbing them, calling to qualify, and converting favorable contacts into a permission-based return path through email and paid digital advertising.
## Key Takeaways
- Before engaging any [[Primary Source]], you must define who your prospect is, splitting the work into a controllable old-school side (a [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]] of call-ready names) and a digital side built on permission-based email lists.
- Rather than chasing a perfect avatar you can rarely reach, anchor your market with a [[Minimum Qualification Standard]] - the smallest prospect you can serve profitably - so bread-and-butter clients keep the business alive while you work toward ideal prospects.
- Set a serviceable distance for your market: only pursue prospects you can actually deliver to, then expand or contract that boundary over time as the business proves itself in [[2.1 Defining Your Market]].
- Specialty markets like histology or industrial valves have small, easy-to-find universes through trade organizations, while general products require shrinking the world down by ZIP code and radius before [[2.2 Putting Together Your Prospect List]].
- Sift and scrub your list through a [[List Scrubbing]] service to catch address changes before mailing, because every list naturally accumulates disqualified prospects as you engage it.
- Direct mail gives little feedback, so calling the scrubbed list surfaces the real decision-maker and qualifies people far better than letters - just confirm your industry's do-not-call rules first per [[Appendix A: Telemarketing Legislation]].
- When a call produces a favorable, qualified contact, ask permission to stay in touch and lift that person onto a [[Plus List]], then nurture them with permission email and follow-up so the spark does not die.
- In the digital space, win emails through reciprocation (free assessments, ebooks, trials), strive for double opt-in to stay legal under the CAN-SPAM act, and use Facebook for ideal-prospect targeting versus Google for capturing people actively searching.
