# 018 - How to Write Opening Lines for a Call Script
## Summary
John and Ted break down how to write the opening lines of a cold-call script, focusing on the functional mechanics rather than persuasion. They split a cold call into three parts: engaging the gatekeeper, reaching the decision maker, and closing. Ted walks through statements of fact (name, company, what you do), a pause, a statement of purpose to "speak with" the buyer, and an "if you even do that" qualifier that lets prospects opt out painlessly. The core argument: build smooth "railroad track" structure that carries you to the decision-point "intersections," accept whatever you hear, and collect useful information along the way rather than fighting objections.
## Key Takeaways
- Open with three statements of fact, your name, company name, and what you do, then insert a deliberate pause so the gatekeeper registers the information; this relates to maintaining a clear [[Call Voice]] and [[Professional Speech]].
- Frame the statement of purpose as wanting to 'speak with' the buyer rather than meet or sell, so you have room to fall back to sending information; this connects to the [[3.1 Call Objectives]] of the call.
- Add an 'if you even do that' qualifier so the prospect can disqualify themselves painlessly, which doubles as a filter that gives you qualifying information without relationship tension; see [[Part 4: Qualifying Information]].
- Treat each decision point as an 'intersection' where all bets are off, and write down the responses you hear over thousands of calls to learn your real objection patterns, supporting [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]].
- When you reach the decision maker, repeat the statements of fact and use a 'don't know' statement plus a short unique value position, then ask a what/when/how question that can't be answered yes or no, mapping to [[3.4 Sample Calls]].
- Practice acceptance: take whatever the prospect says, including illogical objections, and resist correcting them, because operating from abundance rather than scarcity is the healthier mindset covered in [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Use sending material as a bridge rather than a defeat, because offering to mail or email a One-Pager turns calls that produce no finished lead into qualified, mailable contacts; see [[2.8 Sending Mail to Your Plus List]].
- Close calls deliberately with lines like 'it sounds like you're happy' or 'we'll be back with you soon' so conversations that don't naturally end still get sewn shut respectfully.
