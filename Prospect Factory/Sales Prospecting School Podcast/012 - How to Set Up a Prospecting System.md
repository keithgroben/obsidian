# 012 - How to Set Up a Prospecting System
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot interview Ted's daughter Sarah Stevenot of Farming Cards, a real estate direct-mail service, to walk through a six-step framework for setting up a prospecting system (a "prospect farm"). Using real estate, insurance, and digital examples, they argue you should define a manageable target market, list and test outreach channels for traction, build a consistent call/mail schedule, establish a clear value conversion, measure it with ratios and A/B testing, and finally commit to a daily routine. The core message: mix old-school personal contact with new-school automation, stay top of mind, and be patient because farming is a long-term play.
## Key Takeaways
- Step one is shrinking an overwhelming universe of prospects to a workable group, the same discipline as [[2.1 Defining Your Market]]; Sarah narrows a real estate farm to a few streets and screens for a 4-7% turnover rate rather than just cutting by size.
- Drawing on Gabriel Weinberg's Traction, they recommend listing every possible channel, narrowing to three, and testing for a champion, while noting one channel often cascades into others, as with [[Lead Generation]] driven by the phone.
- Your highest-traction channel is not necessarily where you spend the most time; John's Google AdWords ad takes two hours yet drives the sale that weeks of content set up, echoing the synergy ideas in [[4.1 Every Day of Phoning Is a Good Day]].
- Building a consistent cadence prevents the fits-and-starts of dropping marketing once leads arrive, the heart of [[2.4 Call Cycles]] and the call cycles and mail cycles Ted describes in Prospect Factory.
- Automation lets you set scheduled follow-up (anniversary cards, birthday notes, drip messages) and forget it, freeing the seller to spend time talking to qualified people instead of remembering dates, a modern take on [[5.2 Automation and Follow Up]].
- A real value conversion is permission and a return path to stay in touch, not vanity metrics; 100 likes with zero lead-form conversions is worthless, so define what action you actually require.
- Learn your ratios first, like 12 touches to 2 responses or a 10-3-1 close rate, then improve them by layering channels, which is exactly the point of [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]].
- Once you find your champion message, throw challengers at it with A/B testing, but beginners should get a framework working before tuning, since [[Metrics & Ratios]] optimization can become a black hole.
- The sixth step is a daily routine that puts your most powerful outreach first thing in the morning; [[Time Blocking]] reduces the mental cost of task switching and, like the gym, gets easier after a few weeks of Persistence.
