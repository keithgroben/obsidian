# 030 - Sales Prospecting Superpower
## Summary
John and Ted dig into a "sales prospecting superpower" Ted picked up from Dan Kennedy's No B.S. Direct Marketing: the idea of the response threshold. Every offer asks something of the prospect, and that ask should match where the buyer sits on a continuum of evolving interest. They map high, medium, and low thresholds (book an appointment, download a report, watch a video) against the AIDA model, argue that most businesses fail by asking for too much too early, and show how to layer multiple thresholds into one piece. They close with David Frey's 12-step sales-letter template.
## Key Takeaways
- Every offer carries a response threshold, and the central rule is to match the size of the ask to where the prospect sits on the continuum of evolving interest rather than always pushing for the high-commitment action; this is the same instinct behind a phone [[Call Objective]] that opens with a softer fallback.
- High, medium, and low thresholds map cleanly onto AIDA and onto cold-call structure: when the decision-maker is unavailable, conceding to "can I send them something?" drops to a lower threshold the prospect can handle on their own time, which is exactly how a good script feeds [[3.4 Sample Calls]].
- People don't want to be sold, so asking for a phone number is a higher threshold than asking for an email, because email lets the prospect ignore or unsubscribe without confrontation.
- Most businesses fail their ads by asking for too much too early; the fix is to build one piece that offers multiple thresholds at once (call now, download a report, watch a video) so prospects self-identify and engage when ready.
- The fear of giving away free information and losing a ready buyer to a competitor is overblowreciprocity and consistency mean most people who receive value stick with you, echoing the give-first logic behind [[2.8 Sending Mail to Your Plus List]].
- Concentrate richer, multi-touch follow-up on your best 20% rather than spreading thin across everyone, the same economics as moving qualified names onto the [[Plus List]] and mailing them repeatedly.
- Direct-response copy is the model to copy because it is measurable, so emulate what national direct marketers do at a local scale without matching their spend, glossy production, or [[Properties of Mail]] budgets.
- When writing the letter, lead with the prospect's problem (the "bleeding neck") not with yourself, use absolute language, A/B test one variable at a time, and follow Frey's 12 steps from headline through the PS to get out of your own self-talk.
