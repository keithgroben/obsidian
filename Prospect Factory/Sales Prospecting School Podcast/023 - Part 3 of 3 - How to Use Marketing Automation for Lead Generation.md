# 023 - Part 3 of 3 - How to Use Marketing Automation for Lead Generation
## Summary
The third installment of a three-part digital marketing series, this episode explains how marketing automation generates and nurtures leads. Hosts John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot define automation as systems built on triggers and workflows, then walk through a deliberately complex two-path example: one path remarkets, drip-emails, and lead-scores prospects to qualify them, while the other notifies sales, schedules follow-up, and pre-builds quotes. Tying everything to the AIDA model, they argue automation enforces consistent follow-up, removes emotion and human error, and produces data to test and improve. They close with a simple "where to start" playbook spanning SEO, PPC, and one starter workflow.
## Key Takeaways
- Marketing automation is best understood as a system of triggers and workflows: an action like submitting a form fires a cascade of internal notifications and external follow-ups, much like the rudimentary one-card filing systems that evolved into today's [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]].
- Every interaction should require a value exchange before money is spent; the hosts insist on setting a [[Minimum Qualification Standard]] so prospects must put their hand up before you remarket or follow up with them.
- Lead scoring lets you assign points to prospect actions and sort them into interest buckets, which the hosts admit is subjective but testable, echoing the qualifying mindset behind [[3.2 The Five Basic Outcomes to Most Calls]].
- Automation's biggest payoff is guaranteed, consistent follow-up, removing the human error and forgetfulness that lose deals; this systematized persistence is the digital cousin of [[6.4 Persistence]].
- Drip campaigns and automated messages let you always present your best, most gracious self by stripping emotion out of communications, directly easing the burden discussed in [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- The reporting side of automation reveals bottlenecks, conversions, and which content actually works, reinforcing the discipline of [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]] and the 80/20 rule for prioritizing effort.
- Everything maps back to the AIDA model as a cyclical loop, where post-sale survey feedback feeds the awareness stage so the next prospect gets a better experience.
- To start without being overwhelmed, build a WordPress site with Yoast and SSL, pick one niche, do keyword and competitor research, layer hyper-relevant PPC, and set up even a single follow-up workflow in your CRM.
