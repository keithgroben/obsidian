# 031 - Sales Prospecting Letters - A 12-Step Template
## Summary
John and Ted break down David Frey's 12-step "foolproof sales letter" template and the direct-marketing mindset behind it, drawing on Dan Kennedy, Gary Halbert, Joe Polish, and Michael Masterson. Their core argument: a single sales letter that gets traction can become the cornerstone of a business, and the skill is testable, learnable, and transferable across channels. They walk every step in order, attention headline, problem, solution, credentials, benefits, social proof, offer, guarantee, scarcity, call to action, warning, and PS, stressing emotional buying, fear of loss, hidden benefits, and disciplined A/B testing with tracking codes.
## Key Takeaways
- A sales letter must overcome the reader's buying resistance, and the seven universal motivations (wealth, looks, health, popularity, security, inner peace, free time) are best leveraged through fear of loss, which outpulls promise of gain roughly 5 to 1, the emotional engine behind effective [[Properties of Mail]].
- Every mailing needs a tracking code on the tear-off reply form so you can attribute responses to a specific letter version and batch, which is the discipline that makes Sending Mail to Your Plus List measurable instead of guesswork.
- A/B test by changing only one variable at a time, but accept that you are 'wandering into the fog' until a champion letter emerges, the same incremental testing logic that governs an [[Incremental Mailing]].
- Sell benefits, not features: draw a line down the page, list every feature, then translate each into the hidden emotional payoff (a hot tub becomes better communication and a stronger marriage), the gold mine most marketers never dig.
- Length should match interest, not arbitrary limits, a four-page letter outpulls a two-page letter because the buyers are the ones who read to the end, reinforcing that mastering the [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers]] discipline is separate from long-form direct mail.
- Calibrate the offer to where the prospect sits in the buying continuum, leading with a low-threshold lead magnet (a free report or guide) rather than asking for a credit card, which mirrors matching outreach to the prospect's readiness in [[Part 5: The Plus List]].
- Use social proof carefully, because citing a statistic like '1 in 4 people vote in the primary' can backfire and fortify a prospect's inaction; verify testimonials are legit (FTC) and confirm the data moves people in the right direction.
- When you cannot offer a money-back guarantee, guarantee your intent instead, promising honesty, effort, and a clear exit if it is not working, which builds the trusted-advisor relationship that sustains long-term Synergy Between the Plus List and Direct Mail.
