# 043 - How to Effectively Build and Qualify a List
## Summary
John and Ted answer three listener questions about building and qualifying a prospect list. The core argument: the goal of a first call is not to close a sale but to qualify the list. Starting from a compiled list, you scrub it (national change of address, dedupe, do-not-call), then call to confirm legitimacy, fit, and willingness to talk, collecting key data points like contact names and renewal timing. They also explain why they build pauses into scripts: identifying yourself early and pausing respects the prospect, sets realistic expectations, reduces call reluctance, and makes calls more efficient.
## Key Takeaways
- The point of a qualifying call is to refine your list, not to close on the spot. Setting that expectation removes the sting of rejection and makes calling sustainable, which connects directly to [[3.1 Call Objectives]] and [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Always start from a compiled list built on demographic and industry data (SIC codes, geography), then clean it through a national change-of-address pass, dedupe it, and apply your do-not-call list before calling, the groundwork covered in [[2.2 Putting Together Your Prospect List]] and [[List Scrubbing]].
- Calling itself is a sifting tool: confirm the listing is legit, the company actually buys what you sell, and the decision is made within your market, the most basic levels of a [[Minimum Qualification Standard]].
- Even when you don't reach the buyer, capturing a contact name or buying-season data is a real win, notching a prospect up roughly three-to-one and unlocking the value of your [[Mailing Database]] through personalized merges.
- Willingness to engage is itself a qualification: a good prospect must be reachable through your channel and willing to talk, which ties to [[Channel Groups]] and shapes who lands on your [[Plus List]].
- Pauses and early self-identification (saying you are a broker and what you sell, then pausing) make you vulnerable to an early no, but that respect and zero-resistance posture lowers call reluctance and speeds the call, see [[Professional Speech]].
- Knowing key industry-specific data like lease or contract renewal timing lets you sort and prime your list so you call the right people at the right time, a benefit of disciplined Call Cycles and [[2.4 Call Cycles]].
- Over time, respectful, non-hammering repeated outreach warms a list from cold to warm, and detailed selling is best done by a persuasive sales letter sent after qualification rather than dumped on the phone.
