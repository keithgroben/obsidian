# 038 - Sales Prospecting - How to Annex Your Results
## Summary
John and Ted unpack how to "annex" (multiply) your results by finding the one activity that drives everything else. They build a three-part test: an activity must (A) connect you with other people, (B) force follow-on activity, and (C) lead to an offer. Using analogies like a low-carb diet, an ice cube tray, and dealing cards, they argue against perfectionism and vanity metrics (likes, shares) in favor of proactive outreach. The key move is making calls and promising follow-up material now, which forces you to build the rest of the system rather than waiting to perfect it first.
## Key Takeaways
- The episode's central test is whether an activity connects you with people, drives further activity, and leads to an offer, which maps to setting clear [[3.1 Call Objectives]] for every outreach effort.
- Chasing likes, views, and shares produces 'red herring' successes; the real metric is conversion, which ties back to [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]] rather than vanity engagement.
- Proactive, high-control channels like cold calling and [[2.8 Sending Mail to Your Plus List]] beat passive channels like SEO and social media because you initiate the human connection.
- Promising to send a prospect something forces the next activity into existence, the 'ice cube tray' effect that underlies [[4.2 Synergy Between the Plus List and Direct Mail]].
- You cannot tell your whole story in one call or one letter, so build a series of touches over time, which is the logic behind a real [[Call-Cycle]] and [[Mail Cycle]].
- Perfectionism is a disguised form of [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]]; building the perfect website or drip sequence becomes a way to avoid uncomfortable conversations.
- Start with a minimum viable offer (even a three-page PDF) and let early calls reveal what to build, since you can refine [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers]] and follow-up materials over months while you keep dialing.
- Encapsulating a defined offer, such as a seminar with a fixed date, removes the fear of success by bounding what you are promising and feeds [[4.1 Every Day of Phoning Is a Good Day]].
