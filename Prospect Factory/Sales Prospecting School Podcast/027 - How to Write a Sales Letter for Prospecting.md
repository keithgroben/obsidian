# 027 - How to Write a Sales Letter for Prospecting
## Summary
John and Ted break down how to write a sales letter for prospecting, drawing on Dan Kennedy's "The Ultimate Sales Letter," AWAI copywriting material, and a DigitalMarketer article by Katherine Aragon. Their core argument: persuasion fundamentals are unchanged by the internet, so a prospecting letter must first deeply understand the customer's "bleeding neck" problem, then use the word "imagine" to let prospects paint their own ideal outcome. They walk through three copy formulas, stress that every message creates a filter, and recommend long-form copy plus A/B testing across multiple letters for different buyer assumptions.
## Key Takeaways
- Persuasion among humans hasn't changed; the internet only altered the delivery medium and attention span, so the foundational tenets of a persuasive letter apply whether it's mailed or on a screen.
- Before writing anything, sit down and intimately understand the customer's problem (Kennedy's "what keeps them up at night," Marshall's "bleeding neck") and target people who know they have the problem and want it fixed, which connects to [[2.1 Defining Your Market]].
- The single most persuasive word is "imagine" because it lets the reader fill in their own emotional details, painting an idyllic outcome the writer could never script for them.
- Every message you write inevitably creates a filter; leading with cost-consciousness, for example, will collect price-resistant, cheap prospects, which mirrors the segmentation logic behind [[2.2 Putting Together Your Prospect List]].
- Formula one (idyllic picture + bold promise + proof + offer) is the hardest because it demands the deepest understanding of the prospect before painting their ideal life.
- Formula two (introduce problem + agitate + present solution) works like a doctor finding the sore spot, piling on expert testimony to make the pain vivid before offering the cure.
- Formula three (promise + proof) is the most gimmicky and only works on prospects who have already raised their hand, akin to following up a [[Plus List]] contact who self-identified interest.
- In prospecting you're seeking current interest, not an immediate sale, and these letters pair naturally with [[2.8 Sending Mail to Your Plus List]] and being sent in cycles per [[Mail Cycle]] for A/B testing across buyer assumptions.
