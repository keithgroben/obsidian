# 009 - How to Make Sales Follow-Up Easier
## Summary
John Stephenson and Ted Stevenot break down sales follow-up as the disciplined process of moving a prospect toward a sale or respectfully releasing them. They argue most businesses, especially small ones, lose deals not from weak outreach but from "food rotting on the dock"—leads that never get followed up. The core fix is a repeatable system: map a fixed series of meaningful touches (roughly six to eight) per stage, write reusable but personal template messages, and use affordable tools like a CRM, mail, and email drip automation. They also cover lead scoring, content as follow-up fuel, and the danger of over-optimizing.
## Key Takeaways
- Follow-up is best understood as moving a contact forward toward a sale or respectfully releasing them, and building proximity and trust through a planned series of touches rather than ad hoc messages, which connects directly to [[Section 5: Following Up with Finished Leads]].
- Plan a fixed sequence of roughly six to eight meaningful touches per stage so you settle on a reusable stream instead of reinventing every message, which is the heart of [[5.2 Automation and Follow Up]].
- Use a respectful 'release' or number-three letter after about three no-go contacts so prospects drop back to suspect status, keeping emotion out of it as discussed in [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- The biggest follow-up failure is having an abundance of leads with no system to handle them—'food rotting on the dock'—which only a documented process can prevent, echoing [[5.1 Tracking Follow-Up with Status Codes]].
- Write template letters that are well-written, typo-free, and feel personal, like a plain Gmail message with black text on white, an idea that maps to [[5.4 Sample Follow-Up Letters]] and [[5.3 Follow-Up Letters]].
- Physical mail still beats most channels for follow-up because it feels thoughtful and personal, and an affordable CRM can even send mail for you, supporting the case for a [[Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)]].
- Email drip campaigns fire on triggers and conditional logic—time delays or recipient actions—to automate touches without manual reminders, the mechanism behind [[5.2 Automation and Follow Up]].
- Lead scoring and over-optimization can become a black hole of minor changes for minor gains, so begin with a simple system and improve later rather than tweaking endlessly, a caution tied to [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]].
