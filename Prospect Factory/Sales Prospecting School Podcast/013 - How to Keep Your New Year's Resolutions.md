# 013 - How to Keep Your New Year's Resolutions
## Summary
A reflective, off-topic episode where John and Ted tackle New Year's resolutions and, more broadly, how to actually deliver on goals. Their core argument: goals fail without action and systems, so the trick is converting resolutions into routines that strip out willpower. They cover looking back to look forward, writing goals down, grouping small actions into "triads" that support one another, and shrinking any task to one tiny starting step (the "one thruster"). Persistence past the initial suck, proven models, and becoming a better person regardless of outcome round out the discussion.
## Key Takeaways
- Goals drift into disappointment without a plan and action behind them; the fix is to combine a look back at last year with a look forward, then write down specifically what you want to accomplish, echoing the discipline behind [[6.4 Persistence]].
- Routines, not willpower, are what make hard things sustainable; Ted notes he made hundreds of thousands of calls not through grit but by building a process, the same systematic mindset behind [[3.3 General Overviews on Calling]].
- Group small actions into 'triads' (three related steps that prop each other up like cards in a house) so that starting one triggers the next, much like how the conditions for one call set up many in [[3.1 Call Objectives]].
- Shrink any daunting task to a single tiny starting step (one thruster, one phone call) because the hardest part of anything is starting, and once your database is open and phone is ready the second call is easy, as in [[Part 2: Phone Calls]].
- On days a task looks hardest is exactly when you need to show up, since attacking the things you are worst at is what removes the ceiling on your progress, a mindset that directly fights [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Lean on proven models when you are uncertain of the outcome: trusting that an approach worked for others conserves the mental energy you would otherwise burn doubting yourself, which is why knowing [[6.3 Learning Your Ratios]] keeps you on track.
- Even if an effort never reaches its intended payoff, judge it by who you are becoming (more capable, more disciplined), so building capacity counts as an asset at year end.
- Lead generation sits at the base of the goal pyramid, breaking down into call, mail, and electronic outreach, mapping closely to [[The Importance of Prospecting]] as the foundation everything else rests on.
