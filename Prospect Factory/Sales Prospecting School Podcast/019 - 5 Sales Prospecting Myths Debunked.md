# 019 - 5 Sales Prospecting Myths Debunked
## Summary
John and Ted walk through the Rain Group's report "5 Sales Prospecting Myths Debunked," which surveyed 488 buyers and 489 sellers. They debunk five myths: buyers don't want to hear from sellers, cold calling is dead, it's impossible to reach decision makers, buyers don't want to hear capabilities, and cold meetings don't convert. Their core argument is that the five myths map to the full sales cycle, and success comes from doing the homework: surveying the landscape, combining outreach channels, listening and accepting responses, and tailoring capabilities to the buyer's specific problems. The long cut beats the shortcut.
## Key Takeaways
- Prospecting feels hard because it is emotional and rejection-driven, so sellers fall into confirmation bias, looking for evidence that calling fails; building a routine and doing enough volume gives you a real data set instead of a lousy one, which connects to [[6.1 Dealing with Sales Rejection]].
- Buyers most want to hear from sellers early, when they are defining a vision or seeking ideas (71%), and least when finalizing a purchase (around 30%), so reaching out from a [[Primary List]] to learn timing beats arriving late to the party.
- Cold calling is not dead; phone and email are by far the most effective outreach, and combining channels works best, so building [[Channel Groups]] across phone, email, and direct mail covers more communication paths.
- The fallback to send something when you can't reach someone turns one channel into many, which is exactly why every caller should have a The One-Pager ready to mail or email per [[2.7 Mastering One-Pagers]].
- Reaching decision makers is possible through a multifaceted, multi-touch approach that builds a common story and removes obscurity over time, rather than relying on a single interaction or a referral relationship.
- Buyers do want to hear capabilities, but only when framed in primary research relevant to their business and tied to solving their specific problem, which is why qualifying information gathered earlier feeds [[Part 4: Qualifying Information]].
- Cold first meetings do convert when the homework is done; 58% of sales meetings are rated not valuable to buyers precisely because sellers skip the groundwork and pitch features instead of collaborating.
- Referral prospecting is unreliable because it injects a non-business personal bias and limits your reach to who you already know, so it earns meetings without the homework that makes them valuable, connecting to [[4.4 Synergy with Referred Lead Prospecting]].
