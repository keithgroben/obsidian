# 028 - Content Marketing Strategy for Lead Generation
## Summary
In this episode, John Stephenson walks Ted Stevenot through a real B2B content marketing strategy for lead generation, built around a digital case study for a healthcare client selling solvent-resistant slide labels. John argues that effective content is not emotional, story-driven "why" writing but a disciplined procedure grounded in keyword research. He breaks the strategy into three linked parts: a keyword strategy (current, competitive, and gap analysis of core versus long-tail keywords), a content strategy (cornerstone content with internally linked supporting blog posts), and an advertising strategy (single keyword ad groups, match types, and quality score on Google AdWords). The shared goal throughout is capturing qualified leads through a web form.
## Key Takeaways
- A content marketing strategy is a repeatable procedure for moving information from your head to a customer's, not a burst of emotional inspiration; in B2B especially, an emotional 'why' story rarely drives the purchase decision and shouldn't clog up ad copy.
- Start every effort with keyword research in three passes: your current rankings, your competitors' rankings, and a cross-analysis to find gaps where everyone is missing a term that searchers actually use, which becomes your foothold opportunity.
- Distinguish the broad core keyword (slide label printer) from the hyper-specific long-tail keyword (xylene and solvent resistant labels); long-tail terms map closely to a [[Minimum Qualification Standard]]-style buyer intent even when search volume reads as zero.
- Build cornerstone content that packs core keywords plus as many long-tail keywords as possible onto one foundational page, then create supporting blog posts that link back to it; this 'link looping' signals to Google that the content is a complete, relevant thought.
- Gate the value exchange behind a web form following the AIDA model: keep awareness content free, but put pricing, specs, and demos behind a form that captures email, phone, and qualifying information about the prospect, which echoes collecting Qualifying Information for a [[Lead Generation]] pipeline.
- Use single keyword ad groups (SKAGs) on AdWords to avoid Perry Marshall's 'stupidity tax,' bidding on one keyword per ad group with phrase and exact match (and optionally broad match modifier) to control spend.
- Write three ad copies aimed at the informational, comparison, and buyer searcher, then run a champion-and-challenger rotation, killing losers and adding new challengers to continuously improve clicks and conversions.
- Quality score rewards relevancy on both the machine side (keyword in headline, path, description, and landing page) and the human side (high click-through, visitors who stay or click deeper), letting a higher-ranked ad pay less per click than the one beneath it.
