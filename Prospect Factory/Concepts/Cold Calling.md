# Cold Calling

Cold calling is the spine of the Prospect Factory system: a structured, repeatable phoning discipline rather than a personality-driven art. Stevenot frames every call around a defined Call Objective and the recognition that most calls resolve into a small, predictable set of outcomes (the Five Basic Outcomes), so the prospector stops improvising and starts running a process. The script is the engine here — opening lines, the first thing you say, and a temperament tuned for control — and the mechanics of Call Length, Call Pace, Call Voice, and Professional Speech are the dials that make that engine consistent across hundreds of dials.

It matters because the volume game is also a psychology game. Much of the material attacks the friction that kills phoning — fear of rejection, the daily pain of picking up the phone, the myths that talk people out of calling — by reframing rejection as routine and treating "every day of phoning is a good day." Outcomes are something you handle, not fear, which is why the system catalogs 25 call outcomes and exactly how to respond to each.

The pieces connect through a single loop: build and qualify a list, work it with a tested script aimed at a clear objective, sort each contact into a known outcome, and protect the activity from emotional and legal drag (hence the telemarketing-legislation guardrails). Cold calling sits beside cold email as the deliberate, higher-control channel for starting conversations at scale.
