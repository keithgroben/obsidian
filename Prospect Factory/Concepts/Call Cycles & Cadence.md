# Call Cycles & Cadence

A call cycle is the engine that keeps prospects from falling through the cracks. In Stevenot's system, every contact has a defined rhythm of follow-up rather than a single attempt, because the sale rarely happens on the first call. The mechanics live in two simple fields: the Last Call field records when you most recently reached out, and the Current Last Call field flags who is due, turning a sprawling list into a sortable, actionable queue. You always know who to call next and why, without relying on memory or guesswork.

Time blocking is what makes the cycle sustainable. Stevenot treats prospecting as a daily routine with protected, recurring blocks, so follow-up becomes a habit rather than a mood-dependent burst. This is what "takes the pain out of prospecting" — the work is small, scheduled, and repeatable instead of overwhelming.

The pieces reinforce each other: time blocking supplies the consistent hours, the call cycle supplies the structured order, and the Last Call fields supply the memory. Together they form a self-perpetuating loop where activity never stalls and no warm prospect is forgotten — the difference, in Stevenot's framing, between a system that works and the drift that causes people to fail at prospecting.
