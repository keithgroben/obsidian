# Metrics & Ratios

In Stevenot's system, metrics and ratios are what turn prospecting from a hopeful guess into a predictable machine. The core idea from "Learning Your Ratios" is that every prospector eventually settles into stable conversion rates: how many dials produce a conversation, how many conversations produce an appointment, how many appointments produce a sale. Once you know those numbers, you can work backward from an income goal to a daily activity count, so the only variable left to control is effort.

This is why metrics sit at the center of the book's promise. Ratios remove emotion from the work: rejection stops being personal and becomes a line item you already priced in, which is the antidote to call reluctance. Tracking your numbers also reveals which stage actually leaks, so you fix the weak ratio instead of just grinding harder. It is the difference between activity and aimed activity.

The pieces connect through the prospecting system itself. A documented system generates the data; consistent daily activity feeds the ratios; compounding rewards the operator who keeps the inputs steady over time. Knowing your single most important metric lets you build resolutions and routines you can actually keep, because the target is concrete, measurable, and yours.
