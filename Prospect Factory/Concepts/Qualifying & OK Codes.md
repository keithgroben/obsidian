# Qualifying & OK Codes

OK Codes are the heart of how Stevenot turns a raw list into a working pipeline. An OK Code is a single-digit status field (OK Code 1 through 8) stored in a contact's OK Field, recording exactly where a prospect stands the moment a call ends. The codes split into two groups: operational codes mark dispositions that keep a record in motion or retire it (bad number, no answer, not interested, do-not-call), while opportunity codes flag the records worth working again because real qualifying interest surfaced. The Current OK Field always reflects the latest outcome, so the list is self-sorting — you can instantly pull everyone at a given stage instead of guessing who to call next.

This connects directly to qualifying. As you reach decision-makers you collect qualifying information against a Minimum Qualification Standard, and the OK Code captures the result of that judgment. The synergy with one-pagers is the payoff: the one-pager scripts what you say and ask, and the OK Code records what came back, so structure on the way in produces structured data on the way out. Coded outcomes let you apply game-theory thinking to call sequencing and measure which opening lines and list segments actually convert.

Crucially, Stevenot treats this qualifying intelligence as a proprietary asset. The information you collect stays with you — it is the durable equity of the prospecting effort, not something to hand off or expose.
