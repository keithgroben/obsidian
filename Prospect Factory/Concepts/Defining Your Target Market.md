# Defining Your Target Market

In Stevenot's system, defining your target market is the foundational act that everything else in the Prospect Factory depends on—it comes before the list, the letter, or the call. The premise is that prospecting only scales when you stop chasing everyone and instead pick a market you can describe by recognizable, repeatable traits, so the same outreach can be run again and again against a known population.

Stevenot splits markets into two strategic shapes. High-Volume Markets are broad and homogeneous—enough near-identical prospects that you can win on systematized repetition and modest conversion rates. Specialty Markets are narrower and harder to enter, where fit and message precision matter more than raw count. Channel Groups give you the practical handle for either: the categories, industries, and segments you use to slice the universe into addressable, list-buildable groups. Choosing the right shape dictates the volume math, the message, and the data you'll need.

This is where market definition meets list construction (2.2): a sharp definition makes the prospect list a mechanical pull rather than a guess, and clean data lets you refine the segment over time. The same definition then drives the sales letter, the niche-prospecting approach, and the digital/content strategy—each is just the message tuned to a market you've already named precisely.
