# 3.4 Sample Calls

If someone asks me to repeat my name, my company name or what the call is in reference to, it is usually done in a string of questions. From the prospect's side of the line it sounds like this,

*"Can I tell him (or her) who is calling?" (Pause) "And your company name is?" (Pause) "And the call is regarding?"*

These three questions are so commonly grouped together, even if someone asks me just one of them, I generally answer all three immediately,

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and we sell widgets. This is a sales call."*

This moves things along quickly. If the prospect is not interested in a sales call, I want to know it as soon as possible. The sooner I know the prospect is not interested, the better it is for the both of us.

**Start Listening for Signs of Disinterest Right Away**

Even during this early stage in a call, all bets are off on how the prospect will respond. After the opening lines are stated, I immediately start listening for any signs of a not interested or unreceptive response. If the prospect now says, "not interested" for any reason, I will say,

*"Thank you."*

And, let the prospect go.

## Call 1: What to Do When a Prospect Is Not Interested

*"OK" Code for this call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

Let's take a closer look at a call to a prospect who is not interested. In this example, I dial the phone. It rings and someone says,

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

I then say,

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about (or is in charge of, or handles) buying widgets at your company."*

Then, either the person who answered the phone, or a person I am transferred to says something the same as or closely resembling any one of the following responses:

"I appreciate your call, but I'm not interested at this time."
"We already have that."
"We have all we need."
"The business owner's best friend is in the business."
"That's handled by an outside firm."
"We're happy with our current program."
"We're already with AJAX."
"We just bid that out, and no one can beat our current prices."
"I don't handle that, but I can tell you we wouldn't be interested."

To which I respond,

*"Thank you."*

All the above statements express one form or another of non-interest. As stated earlier, I do not argue with a prospect about his or her response. Whether I perceive the response to be logical or illogical, reasonable or unreasonable is of no consequence. I listen for the first sign of non-interest as it presents itself in any form, and when I hear it, I let it go. I move on. I do not stop to write down or enter into the prospect's "notes" field the details of his or her "not interested" response unless I think this information will be helpful to me in the future. Instead, I focus on translating the response into an "OK" Code and moving on to my next call.

## Call 2. What to Do When a Prospect Is Interested

*"OK" Code for this call: "6" (Finished Lead).*

Let's assume on this call that I call a prospect who turns out to have current interest. I dial the phone and the prospect says,

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

I then say,

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about (or is in charge of, or handles) buying widgets at your company."*

"I'll transfer you. One moment please."

(Pause) New voice.

"Bill Smith speaking."

Now I am on the phone with someone who probably is the buyer of my goods or services. But he hasn't said so yet. So, I repeat the opening lines,

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

The person on the other end then answers,

"I handle that. What can I do for you?"

I use the opening lines like a flash card. If circumstances permit, I get to flash the card two times. First, to the receptionist who could have blown me off immediately by saying, "We're not interested." *"OK. Thank you."* And second, I say them to the decision maker. Here, again, I can get shot down quickly. The decision maker may say,

"We've got that taken care of. We're really not interested."

To which I say,

*"Thank you."*

Every now and then someone I am transferred to will be irritated by the fact that I repeat the opening lines. The prospect may respond by saying something like,

"You've got the person, that's why you were transferred to me!"

But I let this pass by without comment. For the 99.9% of the time it is appropriate and helpful to repeat the opening lines, I will live with the less than one percent of the time a few find it redundant.

Assuming I am now talking to the buyer, the next thing I need to know is how interested he or she is in talking about the subject matter of the product or service I am selling.

To find out, I say,

*"I don't know if it would be of help to you, but at XYZ Widget Company, we help customers avoid headaches and save money on their widget systems. I'm calling to find what time of year you might review your widget contracts."*

As before, I have the possibility of hearing disinterest expressed at any time. But, let's say things continue to go well. The voice then says,

"We're looking into that right now. Your timing couldn't be better."

I then say,

*"Great! To provide you with additional information, I'll need to know "X," "Y" and "Z" and it will take me about a week to get back to you. What are your "X," "Y" and "Z" currently?"*

"X," "Y" and "Z" are a few simple questions. The answers to these questions will give me enough information to get back to the prospect with something meaningful soon.

It's possible in some businesses that right now might be a good time to ask for an appointment. For my business, however, it seems to work best to ask a few simple questions, and then promise to contact the prospect again. On the second call to the prospect, after more detailed information such as a price proposal or quotation have been prepared, then an appointment is requested. In Section 5 of this book, we will spend more time addressing follow-up with Finished Leads.

**Even with an Interested Prospect, Keep It Brief**

When a prospect becomes a Finished Lead, I try not to hang on the line exceedingly long asking a lot of detailed questions or engaging in prolonged discussions. What's more, I do little, if any, qualification of a prospect's ability to afford my goods and services on this type of initial prospecting call. That's not to say it can't be done or shouldn't be done, but that I just don't do it. I leave qualifying a prospect's ability to afford my products or services to later follow-up or appointments. You will need to decide what works best for your business.

From here, I quickly go through "X," "Y" and "Z." I confirm a follow-up date such as,

*"I'll call you next week when your quotes are in."*
*"I'll email you the information in the next few days."*

Then I say,

*"I appreciate your giving me this opportunity to be of help to you regarding your widgets. Thanks!"*

I wait for the prospect to reply. I hang up. I designate this prospect as an "OK" Code number "6," and then I call the next prospect on my list. I keep calling even after I have found an interested prospect. All follow-up occurs after I have finished making my calls for the day.

**A Bird in the Hand**

If you make enough calls, you will find interested prospects or Finished Leads, as in this example, right on the phone. They are "birds in the hand." When you have found someone who is currently interested in what you sell and who is willing to favorably consider what you have to offer, congratulations! You are now moving in the right direction.

**Reviewing Phoning, So Far**

The opening lines state:

(1) Who you are.
(2) The name of your company.
(3) The nature of your goods or services.
(4) What the call is in reference to.
(5) And asks, *"May I speak with the person who handles buying widgets at your company?"*

Once you get to the person you are looking for, restate the opening lines. Assuming things continue to go well, I provide an "I don't know if it would help" statement coupled to a brief UVP.

*"I don't know if it would be of help to you, but at XYZ Widget Company, we help customers avoid headaches and save money on their widget systems."*

Follow this statement with a question that will help determine current interest such as:

*"I was calling to find if you have any plans to purchase widgets in the next six months?"*

*"Do you know when you will next consider the purchase of widgets at your company?"*

*"How are you doing these days with your current widgets (or widget provider)?"*

*"What time of year do you usually review your widget purchasing?"*

The prospect will either be interested and/or receptive or not. If the prospect is interested, the door is open to move forward from here. If not, you can part ways with no argument.

If the prospect wishes to provide you with any useful qualifying information, you can accept it. You can immediately put such information in a convenient place (i.e. a "Notes" field, a "Call Back" field or other "specialized" field) where it can be used later.

## Call 3. What to Do When a Prospect Is Not Available

*Possible "OK" Codes for This Call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"2" (Send One-Pager by mail)*
- *"5" (Send Immediate Email)*
- *"3" (Answering Machine, Busy Signal).*

Let's assume now that the prospect being called is not available. In this example, I dial the phone and someone answers,

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company. I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

Then, the person on the other end of the line says,

"I'm sorry, Ms. Jones is in a meeting. Would you like to leave a message or be transferred to her voicemail?"

I then say,

*"Rather than leave a voice mail, would it be OK if I sent Ms. Jones some information through the regular mail?"*

Sometimes I get transferred to the prospect's voicemail before I get a chance to ask about sending information through the mail (i.e. a One-Pager). When this happens, I sometimes call back (or, press "0"), reference who I am again, and say,

*"I just called you a second ago and asked to speak with the person who handles buying widgets at your company. Rather than leave a voicemail message, would it be OK if I sent Ms. Jones some information through the mail?"*

In most "not available" cases, the person on the other end of the line is delighted that I am offering to just "send something." Sending something is perceived to be more convenient for everybody, and a successful screen of the call.

In such cases, the person typically responds,

"Sure, you can send her something. Make it to the attention of Susan Jones. Our address is…"

I take down the follow-up information I need, and I say,

*"Thank you."*

Generally, I ask for at least:

(1) The first and last name of the contact person.
(2) To verify the spelling of the contact person's name.
(3) To verify the mailing address.

I ask for these things even if I already have this information from a prior contact. I do so because contact names and mailing addresses so often change.

Sometimes, in response to the question about sending something through the mail, the person I am speaking to says,

"Can you send him (or her) an email?"

Frequently, people offer up email as a more convenient means of contact. In such cases, I always ask for the name of the person giving me the email address and include in the email message a mention that I spoke to that person and he or she said it was OK to send something via email.

I perceive an email collected from a call like this to be a one-time affair. I will send only the one message that I received permission to send. I do not use this type of permission as a justification to add or "subscribe" a prospect to a long-term email list. If the prospect responds to my single message and wishes to connect with me, then OK. But only after he or she has explicitly agreed to become a true, opt-in, permission-only email subscriber.

**Making Use of Extra Information**

Similar to speaking the opening lines for a call, after I ask the question,

*"Would it be OK to send some information through the mail to the person who handles the purchasing of your widgets?"*

Sometimes the person I am speaking to will say something useful in response that will help me to further qualify the prospect. For instance:

"We just bought 300 cases of what you're selling yesterday."
"We just sold the company and this branch will be closed in two weeks."
"We won't look at our widgets again until next February."
"The boss' roommate is our widget broker."

The person with whom I am now speaking may or may not be a decision-maker, but I try to respect what he or she says regardless.

Most of the above responses would lead me to assign the call an "OK" Code of either "1" (Not Interested Currently) or "7" (Disqualified Prospect) and not pursue sending a One-Pager. The above responses are good examples of how information falls into my hands without my really asking for it. If I think that the information I am being given will be useful in the future, I will make a note of it.

**Sometimes I Can't Get Permission to Send a One-Pager**

Occasionally, after I ask the question,

*"Would it be OK to send some information by mail to the person who handles the purchasing of your widgets?"*

The person I am speaking to will say in response,

"No, you can't send him (or her) a letter."

Sometimes a prospect is so uninterested he or she is not willing to accept a letter of introduction from a salesperson. If so, I'm glad to know it. When this happens, the prospect now becomes an "OK" Code "1" (Not Interested Currently), and I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

**One-Pagers and Answering Machines**

I do not send a One-Pager to a prospect called from a primary list when I only get through to an answering machine or unattended voicemail. I think of such prospects as not available prospects to whom I could send no follow-up. This type of prospect will be given an "OK" Code "3" (Answering Machine, Busy Signal) and be called one more time before the end of the call-cycle.

If I'm calling a prospect already on the [[Plus List]], and I get an answering machine or unattended voicemail, the prospect will remain on the Plus List, and he or she will get a letter through a scheduled mailing. Such a prospect will also be given an "OK" Code "3" and generally receive at least one more call attempt before the end of the call-cycle.

## Call 4. What to Do When a Prospect Expresses Future Interest

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"2" (Send One-Pager by Mail)*
- *"5" (Send Immediate Email)*

Let's assume in this example a prospect who expresses future interest. I dial the phone and someone answers,

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

Then the person on the other end of the line says,

"I'm not looking now, but call me in two weeks, and we can talk about it then."

I treat this prospect like a not available prospect. I ask,

*"Would it be OK if I sent you some information so you could have a way to contact me when you do have time to look into this subject further?"*

Generally, most prospects find this request to be acceptable. In a short-term call back situation like this, I trust that if the prospect is interested, he or she will see the benefit of completing and returning the One-Pager when ready to do so. Depending on the prospect's preference, sending the One-Pager via either email or regular mail works fine.

In cases where a prospect gives me a more definitive or longer-term call back or re-contact date (i.e. months away), I will enter the date into the prospect's "Call Back" or a "Re-Contact" field. At the beginning of each month, I run a query to find the names of prospects who suggested I get back to them in that month.

**Often Future Interest Equals *No* Interest**

Note that prospects who initially express future interest often have no interest but are just reluctant to say so. Some people are uncomfortable having to say "No" to another person under any conditions. I have found it is much better to give such a prospect an easy way out by giving him or her the opportunity to delete an email or throw away a One-Pager in private.

I have also found that a disproportionately high percentage of prospects who say, "I'm busy this week, can you call me next week?" tend to repeat this request week after week. Following up with such prospects is like chasing the wind. In such cases, I shift gears and pursue sending a One-Pager. Sending a One-Pager saves me from having to call back five times before the prospect is finally ready to state his or her true lack of interest.

**Closing A Call**

If you get through to a prospect and you can say,

*"I don't know if it would be of help to you, but at XYZ Widget Company, we help customers avoid headaches and save money on their widget systems.*

*I'm calling to see if you have any interest in looking into pricing information on widgets in the near future (or within the next three months, or six months, etc.)."*

Sometimes a prospect will be receptive enough to tell you when he or she may next look into buying but will not be currently interested enough to become a Finished Lead. When this happens, an effective way to "close" or end the call is to ask,

*"Are you happy with your current provider?" Or,*
*"Are you satisfied with your current service?"*

If the prospect confirms that he or she is satisfied, it creates a natural opportunity to say,

*"OK. Thank you."*

And close the call. Quickly make a note of the date the prospect said he or she might next look into buying and move on to the next call.

## Call 5: What to Do When a Prospect Is Disqualified

*"OK" Code for this call: "7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

A prospect is disqualified after giving you information that establishes under all reasonable circumstances that he or she could never become a customer of yours. This is NOT to be confused with normal lack of current interest. As an example, a disqualified prospect would be a prospect who is not interested in a basement remodel because he or she lives in a home with a slab foundation (i.e. no basement). Under these circumstances, there is no reasonable way to do business with such a prospect.

You already know that removing disqualified prospects from your lists is beneficial because it cleans up your lists. As noted earlier, if you do not add any new names to your list during an update, removing disqualified names will cause your list to shrink. When this happens, it is possible to end up making fewer and fewer calls to a better and better qualified list. However, do not take a legitimate prospect off your list simply because he or she has no current interest. If you are respectful, you can call such a prospect again, and the prospect may be interested in the future.

When I identify a disqualified prospect, I say,

*"Thank you."*

I assign the prospect an "OK" Code of "7," and I later take the prospect's name off my list.

As mentioned earlier, don't forget to keep track of disqualified names because you can cross-list them against new and future lists. By doing so, you can reduce or eliminate the possibility of having to contact a disqualified prospect twice only to find out that he or she is still not a prospect.

## Call 6. "Take me off your list"

*"OK" Code for this call: "8" (Take Off List).*

In this call, the prospect requests that his or her name be placed on my company specific "Do Not Call" list. I dial the phone and someone answers,

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

The person on the other end of the line then says,

"Take me (us) off your list."
"Put me (us) on your 'Do Not Call' list."
"Don't call me anymore."

I then say,

*"Thank you."*

If the prospect asks for further clarification of my contact information such as my name, company name, address and phone number, etc., I will provide it. When the call is finished, I remove the prospect's name from ALL calling lists, and I put the prospect's name on my company specific "Do Not Call" list. (See "Appendix A" on Telemarketing Legislation.)

## Call 7. What to Do When a Prospect Is Hostile

*"OK" Code for this call: "7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

Some calls are hostile or confrontational in the sense that the prospect is so negative, the prospect cannot say he or she is not interested without simultaneously taking a swipe at you. The chief defense in such a call is to accept the person as he or she presents himself or herself, say,

*"Thank you."*

And let the prospect go.

One common theme in confrontational calls is a prospect expressing his or her frustration with telemarketing. Consider the following responses, all of which I've heard in real life situations and at this early stage in a call.

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"You're just a telemarketing jerk!"
"We get three calls a day from sons-of-bitches like you."
"I hate telephone solicitors. It's an invasion of privacy. No one wants to buy what you're selling. You should quit your job."
"If they wanted to talk to you, they'd of f---ing called you."

*"Thank you."*

Certainly, these are hard-hitting responses, but they are extremely rare. In a world where many prospects say,

"I'm not interested, but thanks for calling."

Others may respond differently, and I accept this fact.

If a call is this bad, you may consider taking the prospect off your list (i.e. and adding him or her to your company specific "Do Not Call" list) even though the prospect did not ask you to do so. After all, do you want a future customer who talks to you or other people like that? Besides, if you have a list with hundreds or thousands of prospects on it, there will still be plenty of names left after you take this person off your list. You'll certainly never miss such a prospect once he or she is gone.

## Call 8. "We already have that."

*"OK" Code for This Call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We already have that."

*"Thank you."*

In my early years of phoning, I found this response to be somewhat tricky. If I'm calling to speak with the person who handles widgets, my question itself assumes that the prospect already has or buys widgets currently. So, it doesn't make much sense for him or her to say,

"We already have that."

A few times, I tried saying,

*"Yes, I'm assuming you are a buyer of widgets. That's why I'm calling you! Would it be possible to speak with the person who handles buying widgets at your company?"*

But this only led most prospects to say,

"We're not interested."

In reality, "We're not interested" was what the prospect was actually saying when he or she said, "We already have that."

Once, I even tried saying,

*"Yes, exactly. I realize you already have some widgets. That's why I'm calling to speak with the person who would handle buying widgets at your company. You see, it's like I'm calling to tell you about a special we're having on oil changes for your car and you're saying, 'But I already have a car!' So, yes. I understand you already have some widgets. Would it be possible to speak with the person who handles buying widgets at your company?"*

But this met with no success. Experience has taught me that attempting to work my way past the "We already have that" response goes nowhere. Most of the time, the person is really trying to say that he or she is not interested. There is a certain percentage of the population who will express their lack of interest in this manner. So now, when I hear this response, I accept it. I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let such prospects go.

## Call 9. "We have all that we need."

*"OK" Code for this call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We have all that we need."

*"Thank you."*

This response, like "We already have that," can be especially trying if you are in a business that replaces current products or services. I have attempted to say,

*"Actually, I'm not calling to sell you any more than what you already have. I'm calling to see if there is a possibility of lowering the cost of or improving the service on what you are doing currently."*

Here again, after saying this, most of the time the prospect would follow-up by saying,

"We're really not interested."

The temptation to clarify the situation, with the hope of creating a greater understanding that leads to the discovery of current interest, is generally a lost cause.

Today, I forget about attempting any clarification related to replacement of current services versus the selling of additional services. The prospect's lack of interest is overriding or impairing his or her ability to better understand me regardless of what I say.

So, I accept it. I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 10: "The owner's brother-in-law is in the business."

*"OK" Code for This Call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"The owner's brother-in-law is in the business."
"The business owner's best friend is in the business."
"That's handled by an outside firm."
"We already have an agent (rep, broker)."

*"Thank you."*

Sometimes it's supposed to be important how well you know Jack, right? This type of prospect is a questionable One-Pager candidate at best.

On the phone, however, I just say,

*"Thank you."*

And I let this type of prospect go. On occasion, I've tried saying,

*"I understand that you have a current vendor, but I'm friendly competition to your current vendor, and my company offers what we think is some of the best service available in this area. Would you have any interest in getting a second opinion?"*

To which, most often, the prospect says,

"No, I wouldn't have any interest in a second opinion."

Or worse…the prospect begrudgingly says,

"OK. What have you got to offer?"

Now I get the opportunity to fight an uphill battle against the prospect's current vendor with whom he or she already has a good relationship. This is the kind of prospect for whom I can do a lot of work only to have my recommendations implemented by someone else. I would rather avoid going through that exercise. If the prospect says that he or she already has a good relationship with a current vendor, generally I will believe him or her, and let the prospect go.

There is, however, one variance to a response of this type. Because the prospect has established he or she is, in fact, a buyer of the kinds of goods and services I sell, I might want to put the prospect on my Plus List (i.e. added with an "OK" Code of "1"). If this prospect were already on my Plus List, I would keep him or her on it and continue sending One-Pagers during mail-cycles to cover any outside chance I'm passing up an opportunity. Who knows? Maybe the prospect's current vendor will retire or change careers, thus opening an opportunity for me at some point in the future.

## Call 11. "We don't use brokers."

*"OK" Code for This Call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We don't use brokers."

*"Thank you."*

I have heard this response in an environment where certain "widget" providers won't sell their products *without* a broker. I have even heard this response from a person who was buying from such a provider. Maybe his or her rep just never calls?

In this type of call, I do not engage the prospect in a debate attempting to change his or her opinion about the virtues of using brokers. Instead, I accept the fact that above all else, this prospect is not interested currently.

And, I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 12. "We're satisfied with our current program."

*"OK" Code for This Call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We're happy with our current program."
"We're satisfied with our current program."
"We've got that taken care of."

*"Thank you."*

Each of these statements clearly means that the prospect is not currently interested. This type of response is the easiest to live with. It is straightforward, honest, and clearly stated. I thank the prospect, and I let him or her go.

When a salesperson ignores such a clear-cut response expressing a lack of interest and brings on the "overcome objections" battering ram, that's when trouble starts. Such tactics poison not only the salesperson's own well, but also the wells of every other salesperson on the planet.

When I get a call for a product or service in which I have no interest, I use a response like those above or I say,

*"I appreciate your call, but I'm not interested at this time."*

I then hang up the phone. When a prospect gives me such a response, it's time to fly. I quickly say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 13. "We're With AJAX!"

*"OK" Code for this call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We're with AJAX!"
"We've been with AJAX for over 25 years, and we're not about to change."
"We've used the same widget for thirty years."
"We've been with the same broker for thirty years."

*"Thank you."*

All these responses clearly mean,

"We're not interested."

In one version of this response type, the prospect injects a dose of "patronization" into the response. By "patronizing," the prospect intends not only to state disinterest, but also to take a passive-aggressive jab at the salesperson. It is hard to write or read patronization. You almost must hear it.

*"We're with Ay-Jax!"*

What the prospect is saying is,

*"You dim-witted salesman! We're with AJAX! Don't you know that anyone with AJAX must be thrilled with it? Only fool wouldn't know this! Geez! You just don't get it."*

Here the prospect feels the only way to adequately express his or her lack of interest is to include a swipe at the salesperson. Some prospects figure that a salesperson will only understand "No" if there is a dose of pain included with the response.

Another version of this response type goes like this,

*"We're not interested. We're with the XYZ Association Plan. It's non-profit, and no one can beat our prices. Plus, we just shopped out our widget costs last week and our sales rep is my Aunt Edna."*

In this case, the prospect doesn't think a salesperson will be satisfied to turn away after hearing only one negative response. So, the prospect gives the salesperson a list of negative responses ensuring the clear communication that he or she has no current interest. When this happens, I wait patiently while the prospect goes through his or her list.

Then I politely say,

*"Thank you."*

And, let the prospect go.

## Call 14. "You're wasting your time."

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"You're wasting your time."

This is a rare, yet recurring response. When I hear it, I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 15. "We don't have that."

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We don't have that."
"We don't get those here."

*"Do you have any intention of looking into widgets in the near future?"*

"No."

*"Thank you."*

If I ask the question,

*"Do you have any intention of looking into widgets in the near future (or currently)?"*

Then I'm on the right track. If I hear a "No," I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 16. "We don't need it."

*Possible "OK" Codes for This Call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"At our company 2 + 2 = 3. So, we don't need it."
"We're a Transylvania Corporation. So, we don't need it."
"We only have two left-handed employees. So, we don't need it."

*"Thank you."*

Obviously, all these statements express a lack of current interest. What's notable here is that sometimes a prospect will express a lack of interest with support information based on an entirely incorrect pre-determined set of assumptions (i.e. "At our company 2 + 2 = 3. So, we don't need it.").

The temptation is to help the prospect see the error in his or her assumptions to create or uncover current interest. Unfortunately, most of the times I've tried to help a prospect reevaluate such assumptions; it has only led to a dead end.

To this type of prospect, I quickly say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let him or her go.

## Call 17. "Our home office is on the planet Mercury."

*Appropriate "OK" Code for This Call: "7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"Our home office is on the Planet Mercury."
"We were just bought by a company from Bangladesh."

*"Thank you."*

The point in this call is that the decision to buy is not made in the office you are calling. Call Bangladesh. Call the planet Mercury. Or, take this prospect off your list. If the decision-making office is located outside the physical parameters of your market, then this listing is not a prospect.

To such a prospect I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 18. "We just reviewed that."

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"2" (Send One-Pager by Mail).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We just reviewed that."
"We just bid that out, and no one can beat our current prices."
"We've already got two sales representatives looking at that, and we wouldn't really be interested in having any more look into it."

*"Thank you."*

Sometimes, timing is important. It may be especially difficult to let go of a prospect who says,

"We've already got two sales representatives looking at that, and we wouldn't really be interested in having any more look into it."

This prospect is considering buying something now. The prospect is close to being a Finished Lead, but I am just missing him or her. At this stage, such a prospect would be very hard to win over with words. Today, I think of this kind of prospect as a long-shot candidate for a One-Pager. Though I may send this prospect a One-Pager by mail later in the day, for now I'll say,

*"Thank you."*

And let the prospect go.

## Call 19. "The person who handles that is dead."

*Possible "OK" Codes for This Call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"7" (Disqualified prospect).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"The person who handles that is dead."

*"Thank you."*

This response is extremely rare, but I have heard it several times. My intuition tells me that sometimes this is a rude attempt on the part of the prospect to say something untrue that is both shocking and negative. Once a prospect even said,

"The person who handles that is dead. We're all dead here. The person you are talking to is a zombie."

However, if you make enough calls, you can never be sure. The more calls you make, the greater the probability of encountering a prospect who has recently had someone pass away. So, when I hear this response, I play it safe and say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

In a business-to-business situation, if the prospect's personal name is on my Plus List, I will delete it and try to pick up a new name in the next call cycle.

## Call 20: "Did I call you?"

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"Did I call you?"

*"No sir, I'm calling you. This is a cold sales call."*

"I'm not interested."

*"Thank you."*

Here, as usual, honesty is the best policy. "Did I call you?" is often an invitation to a fight, but I'm not playing. I am as interested as the prospect is in concretely establishing:

1. Whether or not my call is in fact a sales call.
2. Whether or not there is any interest.

Once I clearly cement the fact that this is a sales call, most prospects of this response type will say,

"I'm not interested."

To which I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 21. "I don't handle that, but I can tell you, we wouldn't be interested."

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"2" (Send One-Pager by Mail).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"I don't handle that, but I can tell you we wouldn't be interested."

*"Thank you."*

Long ago it was considered a big aggravation to have a non-decision maker respond to a sales call in this manner. When I was new to sales I might have asked,

*"How can you tell me there is no interest when you do not handle the subject I'm calling about?"*

However, experience has taught me that there is little to be gained in fighting this response. Sometimes, I may ask for permission to send a One-Pager if I think the prospect is qualified. Or, I may decide to send the prospect a One-Pager through the mail independently.

But usually I just say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 22. "That's none of your business."

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"7" (Disqualified Prospect).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"That's none of your business."

*"Thank you."*

This response is yet another invitation to a fight. But as before, I do not play. Once I remember saying something like,

*"Actually, it is my business! I'm in the widget business! So again, would it be possible to speak with the person who handles the purchasing of widgets at your company?"*

But I'm sure you can imagine how far that got me.

Today, I say to such a prospect,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 23. "We can't give out any names on the phone."

*Possible "OK" Codes for this call:*
- *"1" (Not Interested Currently)*
- *"2" (Send One-Pager by Mail).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"We can't give out any names on the telephone."

*"Thank you."*

Time after time, this response comes up at this stage in a call even though I haven't asked for a name. I have merely asked if I could speak with the person in charge. I've tried saying,

*"I'm sorry! I should clarify that I'm not actually asking you for a name. Instead, I'm asking whether I would be permitted to speak with the person who is in charge of buying widgets at your company. Would it be possible to speak with that person?"*

But, what do you think happens next? Right again!

Another version of this response is,

"We don't give out that information."

*"I see, but I haven't actually asked you for any information. I have merely asked if I might speak with the person who handles purchasing of widgets at your company. Would it be possible to speak with the person who handles widget purchases at your company?"*

Sometimes I may ask for permission to send a One-Pager to this type of prospect if I think the prospect is a buyer of my types of products and services. If I do get permission to send one, it will be to the "company name," or "purchasing," or to "management," etc.

Usually though when I hear a prospect give this kind of response, I just say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 24. "Why?"

*"OK" Code for this Call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"Why?"

*"Because I'm a salesman, and this is a sales call. Would it be possible to speak with the person who handles buying widgets at your company?"*

"Why?"

*"Why is this a sales call, or why am I calling to speak with the person who handles buying widgets at your company?"*

"We're not interested."

*"Thank you."*

If you make enough calls you may hear this response a few times. Asking "Why?" in this manner is considered by some to be a clever way to give a salesperson a hard time. It is only effective on a salesperson who has difficulty admitting or who has not been trained to admit that he or she is making a sales call (i.e. from a legal standpoint, this type of salesperson shouldn't even exist anymore). But since admitting I'm making a sales call has always been something I've been in a big hurry to do; the negative aspects of this response do not have much impact.

Some of the prospects who ask this "Why?" question tend to ask it over and over again, even after the question of "Why?" has already been clearly answered. This is done because the prospect is not getting the expected result, which is to fluster the salesperson. Eventually when the prospect sees it's not working, he or she will usually admit that there is no interest. I am yet waiting for a "Why?" prospect to turn out to be interested, but in 30 years, it hasn't happened.

Today, I clearly answer the "Why?" question no more than twice on a given call, and then I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.

## Call 25. "Are you going to send us a check?"

*"OK" Code for this call: "1" (Not Interested Currently).*

"Amalgamated Freight & Forwarding."

*"My name is Ted Stevenot. I'm with the XYZ Widget Company, and I'm calling to speak with the person who makes decisions about buying widgets at your company."*

"Are you going to send us a check?"

*"No, this is a sales call. I'm calling to speak with the person who handles purchasing widgets at your company."*

"We're not interested."

*"Thank you."*

This response asks for a more concrete clarification that my call is, in fact, a sales call. Once I provide this clarification, then the call can move forward. This prospect is not interested at this time.

So, I say,

*"Thank you."*

And, I let the prospect go.
