# 3.3 General Overviews on Calling

Hidden Interest Versus Interest that Is Loud and Clear

As I engage in the process of assessing the responses I have heard from prospects over the years, there are many responses I have heard repeated. There have been times in the past when I tried to tailor my responses to some of these recurring responses to uncover interest that might otherwise have been overlooked.

Unfortunately, and you will see this in the sample calls, such efforts to uncover hidden interest have usually met with poor results. A recurring truth is that genuine interest needs little help in being uncovered. In most cases, it comes through loud and clear, and it is easily recognizable on its own.

[[Call Length]]

In the sample calls ahead, you will note that as I make calls, I try not to engage in in-depth conversations with prospects on the phone. This is especially true with prospects who are not interested. Instead, I try to get out of a prospect's way as quickly as possible and with as few words as possible. While I try to collect meaningful information, as noted earlier, I collect it as it falls into my hands.

Occasionally, I may ask one or two extra qualifying questions, but I will only do this with prospects who reveal themselves to be receptive. Even then, I ask very few extra questions. I have seen friendliness turn into unfriendliness quickly, and I assume a prospect's patience to be limited. I try not to force a courteous prospect to change into something less than courteous because I linger on the phone with him or her too long.

When a prospect reveals he or she has an interest in or is qualified for a different product type than I initially called about, I generally pivot to sending more information via One-Pager. Depending on the topic, I may apply an Opportunity Code to such a call.

[[Call Pace]]

When I make calls, I call one prospect after another until I am finished with the total number of calls I plan to make for that day. If you sat next to me while I made calls, you would notice that I run through my calls at a lively pace, and I try to keep moving.

I do not stay on hold for long (i.e. no more than about a minute), and I do not set down the phone between calls. When I call, I wear a headset. I generally do not remove it until my calls are finished for the day, or until I take a break.

While I make a conscious effort not to speak too quickly, I try to work through my calls quickly. Depending upon the circumstances, I generally dial the phone in a range of 50 to 60 dials-per-hour. At first, you may not be able to move quite this rapidly but try to anyway.

[[Time Blocking]]

One of the reasons I have been successful as a prospector is that I designate specific time each day to make calls. I very seldom let anything distract me during this time. Establishing a daily "time block" for calling keeps me in a routine. Having such a routine makes the overall task of calling easier.

Set aside specific time for calls and eliminate interruptions. Let others know that during your "appointment with the phone," you are unavailable. Once you are set up to call, try to see how long it takes you to get through a couple hundred calls without distractions.

[[Call Voice]]

Call "voice" refers to how you sound on the telephone. In terms of how you sound, try not to speak with a voice that is either too excited or too dull. Instead, use a moderate tone of voice that is clear, even-toned and authoritative. Consider the following:

- Clear speech is the opposite of mumbling.
- Even-toned speech is recognizable in that it neither begins nor ends a sentence on an "up" tone.
- Authoritative speech is recognizable in that it tends to end a sentence or a group of sentences on a "down" tone.
- Professional speech is the combination of all three elements of speech mentioned above.

Listen to a responsible professional speak with authority on a subject about which he or she is an expert and you will recognize professional speech instantly. While not overbearing or cocky sounding, it has a ring of certainty to it that inspires confidence. Listen for examples of professional speech in formal speeches, public and network radio newscasts, financial reporting, and press briefings.

[[Call Temperament]]

The last general overview on calling to consider is call "temperament." Call temperament refers to the atmosphere of a call. In this prospecting system, the atmosphere of all calls is no-pressure and non-confrontational. It is never called to "overcome" objections, debate with, or oppose prospects about their responses.

Instead, just relax and go with the flow.

Accept prospects exactly as they present themselves. Focus your attention on assigning "OK" Codes and controlling your overall pace. If you find a prospect who is not interested, just say "Thank you!" Hang up the phone and move on.

Key Terms to Remember

**[[Call Objective]]** – The intended result from a call.

**Call Length** – How much time you spend on a call.

**Call Pace** – How quickly you move from one call to the next.

**Time Blocking** – Setting aside specific, uninterrupted time for calling.

**Call Voice** – How you sound on the telephone.

**[[Professional Speech]]** – Speech that is clear, even-toned and authoritative.

**Call Temperament** – The atmosphere of the call (i.e. non-confrontational, no-pressure).
