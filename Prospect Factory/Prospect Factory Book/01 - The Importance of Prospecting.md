# The Importance of Prospecting

It may come as a shock when you first realize it, but if you are a salesperson, own a small business, are the founder of a startup, or are responsible for the upkeep and growth of almost any legitimate enterprise, you are in the lead generation or "prospecting" business.

Whatever product or service you bring to the market, finding new and potential customers is one of the most important – if not *the* most important – challenges you must overcome to achieve success.

There is no such thing as a successful salesperson, business, non-profit, or other significant enterprise that has not somehow solved the problem of prospecting. Many people who quit sales or were forced to give up on new business they started were good, hard-working people who had fine products and services to sell.

All wanted to succeed, but ultimately, they did not.

The rationalizations for failure seem endless – undercapitalization, market shift, timing, competition, technological challenges, lack of training, poor economy, disruption, bad luck, and more.

While some of these excuses may occasionally prove true, upon further review, the real reason most failures occur is because of the inability to find enough new, good-quality sales prospects before funds run dry.

Assuming you conduct yourself in a professional manner and you sell a quality product or service that is credibly priced, the challenge is clear:

*Before the clock runs out, you must let enough qualified prospects know you exist and that you are willing and capable of serving them.*

Carried out effectively, prospecting informs, establishes trust, and builds bridges to new customers. Its promise is a consistent and generous supply of new buyers that will sustain and grow your business over time. When enough prospects regularly turn to you for help, you will have taken an enormous step toward creating and sustaining long-term business success.

Conversely, running out of prospects is like running out of air.

Even an established business can fail due to ineffective prospecting. It is well known that nothing in life or business remains static for long. Things either grow or they decay.

To keep success over the long-haul, a salesperson or business must be able to generate enough prospects to replace business lost through attrition and to ensure overall sales volume continues to grow. Otherwise, the future of the sales career or business is put at risk.

## Fifty Is Better Than Two

The better the prospecting is, the more successful the salesperson or business will be.

Having enough prospects is like playing poker with a stack of 50 chips versus being down to your last two chips. When you have 50 chips, two don't seem all that critical. But, when you're down to your last two, each chip seems *extremely* critical.

When a salesperson has a *limited* number of prospects:

- The pain from sales rejection is magnified.
- Too much is expected from poor prospects.
- Sales leads seem scarcer than they really are.
- It causes unreasonable fear of the loss of current business because of the high degree of difficulty in replacing it.
- It causes a tendency to oversell the few leads available and makes it even more distressing when a potential sale is lost.

When a salesperson has an *abundance* of prospects:

- It greatly increases confidence in the chance for success.
- It lowers overall sales pressure and reduces stress.
- It is easier to let go of weak or marginal sales opportunities.
- It is easier to be objective and pursue the best interests of prospects.
- It promotes growth and establishes a firm foundation for long-term success.

A salesperson with a generous supply of prospects who loses or misses a sale can think,

"This prospect didn't end up becoming a customer, but that's OK because I know some of the other prospects on my long list of prospects will buy!"

Versus thinking in desperation,

"If this prospect doesn't buy, then who will? This is one of my only leads. Where will I find more potential buyers? I just have to close this sale!"

## The Purpose of This Book

This book describes how to set up and operate a prospecting system that consistently produces large numbers of qualified sales leads. I developed this system during my thirty-year career as a salesman, business owner, and sales prospector. I have used all the techniques recommended in this book with repeated success.

The prospecting system described in this book takes a systematic approach to prospecting and networking. It is NOT based on intangibles such as where you grew up, what you look like, who you know, how charming your personality is or any of the other uncontrollable factors so often considered prerequisite for success.

This book will help you see and understand that smart, routine-based activity is the major difference between succeeding or failing in sales and business.

- It will show you how to do more with less energy.
- It will help you establish and keep momentum.
- It will help you make sales calls without fear.
- It will show you how to not take it personally when your approach is rejected by unreceptive or uninterested prospects.

This book will help you gain control of your sales career. It will help improve the quality of your life as a sales and businessperson. It will show you how prospecting effectively is the key to getting what you want most – to be successful.
