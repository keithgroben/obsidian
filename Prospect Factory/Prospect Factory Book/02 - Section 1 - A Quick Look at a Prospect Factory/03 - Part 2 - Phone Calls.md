# Part 2: Phone Calls

One effective means of collecting primary research, where permitted, is to make telephone-prospecting calls to the prospects on your list. Telephone-prospecting calls are used in this system for the purposes of:

1. Finding currently interested prospects.
2. Qualifying prospects.

For telephone-prospecting to be successful, you must make a consistent and sustained effort to make a sizeable enough to cover ==[?: either your entire market or a manageable segment of that market]==.

Enough time specifically dedicated to calling must also become a part of your regular work schedule. The amount of time required to make calls will be determined by:

1. The total volume of calls you need to make (i.e. determined by the size of the market you intend to prospect).

2. How quickly you can make calls (i.e. how many dials-per-hour you can make and the average length of time you spend on each call).

3. How often you plan to "cycle" or repeat calling through your list (i.e. once every 90, 120, 180 days, etc.).

Allocating time to make calls can occur on a variety of schedules such as daily (i.e. every day between 9 - 11a.m.), weekly (i.e. first two days of the week), monthly (i.e. first week of the month), quarterly (i.e. first month of the quarter), etc.

*See Appendix A regarding telemarketing rules and legislation.*

The speed or quickness at which you can make calls is derived from a combination of:

- Dedicating specific, uninterrupted time for calls – no checking email, social media, or taking other calls.
- Being organized when you call by having your list of prospects "call-ready" – pre-selected and ready to go.
- Following a pre-determined call script.

Later in this book, we will discuss ways to optimize the total volume of calls you make and the speed at which you make them.
