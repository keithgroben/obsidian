# Part 6: The One-Pager

The sixth part of this system sends direct response material to regular mail – yes that's right, I said it, old-fashioned post office snail-mail – to the prospects on your [[Plus List]]. This material is sent on a recurring schedule such as once every 60, 90 or 120 days, etc. The collateral used in mailings is called a "One-Pager."

A One-Pager is a one-page letter or form that carries a brief message about a product or service along with a general description of your business. A One-Pager is not intended to directly solicit a purchase. Rather, it is optimized to seek current interest.

One-Pagers include your business' contact information and a proactive response prompt such as, "For more information, complete this form and return it by email, mail or fax." One-Pagers are used to prospects as a means of re-contacting you when they wish to learn more about your products or services.

Also... I can already hear some readers, "But wait, what about email?!" I promise, I'm not a Luddite and we'll talk about email later. For now, however, here's the basic gist: The only kind of email marketing list to which you should be regularly sending anything is an opt-in permission-only list. Such lists presume that the people on them already know you, have a relationship with you, and – critically – have given you permission to stay in touch.

Our concern here is with generating *brand-new* prospects. Sending marketing email to brand-new prospects who don't know you and without permission is SPAM and a bad idea. Opt-in email lists are powerful tools and serve a useful purpose, but they exist downstream from this initial point.
