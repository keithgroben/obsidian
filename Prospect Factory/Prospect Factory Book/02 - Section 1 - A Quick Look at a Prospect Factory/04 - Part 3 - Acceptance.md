# Part 3: Acceptance

The third part of this system also relates to telephone-prospecting and calls. The aim of this part of the system is not so much about engaging in an action as it is about engaging in an attitude. This attitude pertains to the way in which telephone-prospecting calls are made. The most powerful tool for enacting any anticipated pain from telephone-prospecting can be found in one word: acceptance.

Acceptance in this context means allowing a prospect to respond to your call in any way the prospect desires without ever trying to change the way he or she responds. A prospect will either be receptive to your approach or not. When any indication is given that the prospect is not receptive, simply say "Thank you" and let the prospect go.

By accepting prospects exactly as they present themselves, the process of making telephone-prospecting calls becomes less painful for both the salesperson and the prospect. The prospects who are interested and receptive will be embraced. All other "not interested" prospects are free to go their own way without any argument.

Calls are made with this system as if they were a job on an assembly line. No false expectations. No regrets. Effort and understanding are applied, and everything else takes care of itself. Later in this book is a description of how to make the telephone-prospecting calls used in conjunction with this system. It includes a discussion of scripts, a look at several sample calls, and a brief outline of the kinds of responses typically encountered.
