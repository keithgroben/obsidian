# 2.6 Keeping Your Lists Updated

## Use Information to Improve Your List

After you make calls to your whole list once, and therefore have completed a call-cycle, you should scrub and clean up your list again. Use information you collected during the call-cycle to weed out listings that are no longer viable. Also, sort for duplicates that occur due to changes you recorded while phoning (i.e. changes in contact information, address, company name, etc.).

It is important to realize, if you add no new names to your list during an update (or, at the end of a call-cycle), it will tend to shrink. This happens because you will almost always be removing disqualified listings during an update. Ironically, this tendency for your list to shrink is at least partially good news because the overall list is becoming more streamlined and better quality.

## Growing Your Plus List

As time goes by, you should find that the combination of primary research and outreach through phone calls feeds and expands your [[Plus List]]. Though I routinely end up setting aside disqualified names from my Plus List at the end of a call-cycle, I am generally able to achieve a net gain in [[Plus Listing|Plus Listings]].

This net gain occurs because I generate enough new Plus Listings through the combination of research and calls made to my primary list every cycle. If you find that you have achieved a net loss in Plus Listings at the end of a call-cycle, try to make a concerted effort to find more names to add to your primary list. Otherwise, you may start phasing yourself backwards.

## Tracking Disqualified Names

As you have already seen, there are numerous reasons why a prospect can become disqualified.

Here are a few common examples:

- A prospect goes out of business.
- A prospect moves to an inaccessible location.
- A prospect's business is sold to someone in an inaccessible location.
- A prospect establishes for any other good reason that he or she will never be a buyer of the kinds of products you sell.

Every year, I identify many prospects that are disqualified. I carry forward their names from year to year in my [[List Scrubbing]] database. It can happen over time that this database grows substantially in size and ends up including many irrelevant names.

One way to manage this condition is to send your list out to be checked for "change of addresses" externally. A quick online search for companies that can help you with this will reveal vendors who provide this service for a small fee.

Perhaps a bit aggressive, but if addresses come back undeliverable as a result of such a screening, I toss them. You will have to experiment to find what works best for you. (Note: I keep my in-house Do-Not-Call list separate from this process. These listings are carried forward as stipulated by law. See "Appendix A on Telemarketing Legislation.)
