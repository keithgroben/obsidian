# 2.8 Sending Mail to Your Plus List

## How One-Pagers Are Sent to Prospects

One-Pagers are sent by regular mail to prospects via three general pathways:

- As a result of primary research. (i.e. A prospect on your primary list is upgraded after being further qualified through personal observation or research.)
- As a byproduct of a call-cycle. (i.e. A prospect is called during a call-cycle and a decision is made to send the prospect a One-Pager. A One-Pager is sent to the prospect the very same day.)
- Through a routinely scheduled and recurring mail-cycle.

## Planning the Properties of Your Mail

The properties of mail refer to its physical characteristics such as the kind of envelope used and the collateral inside it. I do not send direct mail with particularly elaborate properties. I use plain white #10 envelopes, and my One-Pagers are printed in black and white on plain, copy-grade paper. I use #10 envelopes because they are common and inexpensive. They are also postal service friendly, meaning they run through the post office's sorting machines smoothly and are easy for carriers to handle.

I also use #10 envelopes because they are printer friendly. I print my direct mail envelopes in-house, and due to this fact, my most important criterion for purchasing a laser printer is how quickly and how well it prints and feeds envelopes. I print envelopes in black and white, though color is OK if you can print it affordably.

I do not pre-print my company's logo on envelopes prior to preparing a mailing, nor do I use mailing labels. Instead, I "paste" my company's logo into an envelope template inside my mailing software and then save it. This enables the software to print my company's logo, return address, and the address of the prospect all in the same pass.

## Defining a Mail-Cycle

A "mail-cycle" is simply how often, or the time period in which, you plan to send a One-Pager by mail to your entire [[Plus List]]. For example, you may wish to send a One-Pager by direct mail to your [[Plus Listing|Plus Listings]] once every 120 days. Therefore, you would be operating with a "120-day mail-cycle."

## Concurring Call and Mail-Cycles

The length of a call-cycle usually matches up with the length of a mail-cycle. Hence, a "concurring call and mail-cycle" is a call-cycle and a mail-cycle that occur within the same time frame.

- The two cycles match up conveniently because at the end of each call-cycle, the Plus List will be updated.
- The updated Plus List at the end of a call-cycle becomes the basis for the mailing list used in the next mail-cycle.
- By matching up the call-cycle with the mail-cycle, you will be able to send mail to the most current version of your list.

Sometimes, such as during a major list update, I may send prospects mail only so I can work on other areas of my business that need my attention. However, almost every call-cycle gets matched up with a mail-cycle. Therefore, my call-cycles are almost always concurring call and mail-cycles.

## Mailing in Increments

I send mail to Plus List prospects in increments or blocks. For example, if I have grown my Plus List to include 3,000 prospect names and I plan to send letters in a 90-day mail-cycle, I will divide my list into three parts. Thereby, I will send 1,000 letters every 30 days until the end of the cycle.

One reason I divide up my list in this way is to prevent getting back too many Finished Leads at once. Mailing in increments spreads out the number of responses I receive. This prevents overflows which tend to complicate and delay follow-up. The older a lead gets, the less viable it becomes. So, it is always a priority to get back to interested leads as soon as possible.

## Saving Money on Postage by ZIP Code

Another reason I send mail in increments is to attain favorable postage rates. In general, grouping more letters from the same ZIP code together in one mailing tends to lower costs. To accommodate this, I will do a ZIP Code sort of my mailing list before I divide it into increments.

If you choose to send bulk or presorted-standard mail, the US Postal Service has specific requirements about when and how to certify and update your mailing list for address and zip code accuracy. Visit USPS.com online or check with the Postal Business Center nearest you for more details on permits and other requirements for sending presorted mail.

## Dovetailing Call and Mail-Cycles

Prior to the start of a concurring call and mail-cycle, I will pre-write at least two One-Pagers. I send both versions throughout the concurring cycle. One version is used for the calls and one version is used for the mail. The version for calls is sent to any prospect called who becomes an "OK" Code "2" (Send One-Pager by Mail) or "5" (Send Immediate Email).

As I work, I allow the two platforms to operate independently. As a result, some prospects will be contacted or receive One-Pagers more than once within the course of the overall cycle.

For example:

- A Plus List prospect may be contacted directly by phone.
- He or she may receive a One-Pager as a result of the call. Such as a prospect who becomes designated as an "OK" Code "2" (Send One-Pager by Mail) or "5" (Send Immediate Email).
- With a few exceptions, *all* prospects on the mailing list (i.e. Plus List) will also receive a One-Pager by direct mail.

Though some prospects may receive two One-Pagers during a concurring cycle, they will receive a different One-Pager each time.

## Creating and Managing Your Mailing List

Every mail-cycle I create a new mailing list. Therefore, every mailing list used in a mail-cycle is only a temporary list. This happens because the Plus List is the permanent and controlling list, and the mailing list exists a step beneath it. At the end of a call or mail-cycle, the old mailing list will be archived and replaced by the latest version of the Plus List.

At the end of a call-cycle, I export a copy of my Plus List into the mailing software I use (i.e. once it has been re-certified to meet the post office's requirements for bulk mailing). I keep this new mailing list separate from the original Plus List database.

## Mail List Scrubbing - Adding and Removing Names

Most addition and deletion of prospect names to a mailing list will happen at the end of a call or mail-cycle. However, there are several instances in which names may be removed from a current mailing list during a mail-cycle.

I will consider the following factors before I print an incremental mailing within a mail-cycle (i.e. again, in a three-month mail-cycle, an increment would be any one month within the three-month period). I will use all relevant information I have at my disposal from recent research, calls, quotes, or sales activity to help me decide which prospects to remove from my current mailing list.

I will remove a prospect's name from a current mailing list if:

- As a result of recent calling or other activity, the prospect has become a disqualified prospect.
- The prospect has recently requested his or her name be removed from my mailing list.
- The prospect has recently become a client.
- As a result of recent calling, I designated the prospect as a new "OK" field "4" (i.e. Disconnected Number, or Major Address/Contact Change).
- As a result of recent calling, I designated the prospect as an "OK" field "6" (i.e. Finished Lead).
- I received a completed One-Pager from the prospect within the last 90 days.

This kind of cross-reference work should be done back to your phone list, as well. If you know of any names on your Plus List or primary list that have been recently quoted, sold or disqualified as the result of non-call activity, then you may wish to temporarily or permanently suspend calling such prospects.

## Thinking About Frequency of Contact

In most cases, it is exceedingly difficult to predict exactly when a prospect will become interested in buying a product or service. Because of this, I try to stay in touch with prospects in a persistent and professional way, hopefully without overdoing it.

The question of, "How often is too often?" to call or send mail to a prospect is one you'll have to answer on your own. One of the key elements of this system is to stay in touch with prospects and be more easily accessible to them by presenting an increased number of low-pressure messages over time. The low-pressure element of this approach should help insulate you from aggravating a prospect because the prospect feels you are contacting him or her too much.

If a prospect feels like I am contacting him or her too often, he or she will generally let me know. If such incidences happen infrequently, you are probably OK. But if they happen regularly, you should give some thought to lengthening your call and mail-cycles.

## Key Terms to Remember

**[[Mail Cycle]]** - The time period in which you plan to send a One-Pager by mail to all your Plus List prospects.

**[[Properties of Mail]]** - These are the physical characteristics of the mail you send - such as the size of envelope, ink color, and paper stock.

**[[Incremental Mailing]]** - An incremental mailing is any individual month within a mail-cycle. As an example, a three-month mail-cycle would include three incremental months. Roughly the same number of letters are sent in each increment.

**[[ZIP Code Sort]]** - Sorting a mailing list by ZIP code is a good way to help achieve the most favorable postage rates. First, sort by Zip code, then divide your list into increments.
