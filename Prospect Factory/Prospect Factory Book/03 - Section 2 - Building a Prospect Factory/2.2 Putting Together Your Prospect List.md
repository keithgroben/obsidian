# 2.2 Putting Together Your Prospect List

## Putting Together an Initial or "Primary" List

From this point through the rest of the book I will refer to what I have heretofore been calling an "initial" list as a "primary" list. Technically, an initial list is only "initial" the first time you use it. Over time, it is more precise to think of such a list as a "primary" list.

==So, once you have formulated a clear idea of who buys your goods and services and where those buyers are located, you will need to construct a primary list of prospects existing within the parameters you have defined.== As it relates to telephone-prospecting calls, the better quality the list, the fewer calls you will need to make to find Finished Leads.

## Developing Your List

Each year I try to prepare one primary list to work with throughout the whole year. I spend at least several weeks in collective time annually, researching and maintaining my list. During maintenance I sort, clean-up, screen, and add new names, where available.

The first time you prospect you will start out with at least one list. As you work, you will create subcategory lists that are by-products of the original list. You will carry forward these subcategory lists from one year to the next. Simultaneously, you will bring in new potential prospects as time goes by.

## Finding Primary Sources for Prospects

To collect names for your primary list, you will need to look at some "primary sources." The most basic example of a primary source is a public telephone directory. Other primary sources include reputable online list vendors, local business directories, Chamber of Commerce directories, regional, professional, or industry-related directories.

Try going to a public library and asking about lists. You will be amazed at how many lists and directories are available if you will only ask to see them. You may be lucky to be in an industry having a single directory containing all or most of the names of your potential prospects. If not, you will likely end up working with several primary sources to compile your primary list.

## Professional List Companies

If you are not interested in putting together a primary list on your own, you may wish to employ the services of a professional list company. There are many companies in the list business. Typically, such companies compile a list based on criteria you provide and then either sell or rent the resulting list back to you in the format of your choice. Some online list services allow access to cloud-based lists for a monthly or annual subscription fee. One advantage of cloud-based services is their ability to frequently update their data.

## Selecting Prospects for Your Primary List

As you study your primary sources, apply the "who" and "where" criteria you established when you defined your market to help you select which prospects to approach. In business-to-business prospecting, finding personal contact names is not generally necessary in advance because such names can be drawn from the process while you are calling.

Usually, there are so many names to go through during the list selection process it is difficult to deeply consider the probable viability of any individual prospect. At a certain point, you must decide about whether a listing looks like a prospect and keep moving. Be aware that some people overanalyze prospects before calling them as a form of procrastination. Try to avoid that trap.

Depending on how detailed your primary sources are, you may need to make some educated guesses from time to time as you select your primary list. A company named "Designs Amalgamated, Inc.," may sound like an architecture firm, but when you contact them, you may discover they are a hair salon. This happens occasionally on most lists, so don't be surprised if it happens a few times on yours. When available, using SIC codes (i.e. Standard Industrial Classification codes) to help you select listings can reduce this problem.

## Scrubbing Your List for Duplicates

When you finish selecting your primary prospect list, "scrub it" for duplicates as thoroughly as you can. Whether a primary list or later a [[Plus List]], you can search for duplicates in many ways. Minimally, search by company name, phone, and address. Take care when sorting for duplicates by address because everyone in the same office building does not necessarily work for the same company.

As careful as you are about this, a few duplicates may still get through. I have found instances where companies list separate names, addresses and phone numbers all for the same company. If a company on my Plus List or primary list has several separate listings, I will make a note of the names of those listings in my database. During an update, I mark or tag the duplicate listings on my primary sources accordingly. Database creation and list maintenance are covered in more detail in later sections.

## Follow Copyright Rules and List Rental Agreements

As a word of caution, it can be problematic from a copyright standpoint to enter information from other people's published directories or lists into your own computer database without the directory or list owner's consent. Should you buy, rent or use a published list, be sure to learn and follow any pertinent rules about copyrights.

Some list rental or purchase agreements may only allow you to call through a list once. A list rental agreement may permit that once you have spoken with a prospect and/or the prospect has agreed to follow-up of some kind, then you may enter that prospect's name into your own database. List services are usually very forward about their guidelines, so make sure you understand the rules in advance.

## Key Terms to Remember

**[[Primary Source]]** – An unscreened list of potential prospects such as a business directory or phonebook.

**[[Primary List]]** – A prescreened list of first-level prospects that you select based on how well you believe the names match up with your buyer description.

**[[List Scrubbing]]** – The process of sorting and removing unwanted names from a list such as duplicates, Do-Not-Call listings, or other disqualified names.
