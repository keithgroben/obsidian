# 2.4 Call Cycles

## Call-Cycles

A "call-cycle" refers to the time period in which you plan to call through your entire prospect list. For example, you may plan to call through your list as often as once every 90 days. In this case, you would be working in a "90-day call-cycle." Calling a prospect once every 90 days may be too frequent for some businesses, while too infrequent for others. It will be up to you to define the time parameters of your call-cycles so they are respectful to your prospects and function effectively for your business.

## Managing Calls with "OK" and "Last Call" Fields

The "OK" field houses the coding and tracking system alluded to in Section One which helps manage follow-up with your various prospects. The numerical codes used in conjunction with the "OK" field direct follow-up activities and tell you:

- If a prospect is or has recently become a Finished Lead.
- Whether to send a prospect a One-Pager.
- Which One-Pager to send to a prospect depending on his or her response.
- Whether to remove a prospect's name from your list in the future because he or she is now disqualified.

The "OK" field works in conjunction with another data field, "Last Call," which keeps track of the last date a call was placed to a prospect. In the next section, we will discuss the "OK" field in more detail. For now, however, you need to know that every time you start a new call-cycle you will add two new data fields to your database.

Label these two new columns carefully, such as,

- "OK 2/xx" – Meaning, "OK Field, 2nd [[Call-Cycle]], Year 20xx."
- "LC 2/xx" – Meaning, "Last Call Field, 2nd Call-Cycle, Year 20xx."

These names label the fields as "OK" and "Last Call," and they note the time period in which the call-cycle occurs. In a new call-cycle, all "OK" field data and "Last Call" dates will be recorded in the "OK" and "Last Call" fields that correspond with the new cycle.

## Daily Operations in a Call-Cycle

Each day, I begin early in the morning by sorting my list by "Last Call" date. I use an "ascending sort" (lowest to highest) of the "Last Call" field. In this way, prospects who have not yet been called during the current cycle show up first. As they are called, new information is added to their Last Call and OK fields.

After EVERY call,

1. A number is entered in the "OK" field that categorizes what happened on the call.
2. The date the call was made is entered into the "Last Call" field.

## Your First Call-Cycle

The first day you start working this system you may have, for example, 500 or 5,000 prospect names on your primary list, but no prospects on your [[Plus List]]. At the end of your first day of calls, however, you will surely have found some of your first Plus List prospects.

When your first day ends, you will need a means of knowing,

- Which prospects need to be upgraded or downgraded?
- Which prospects need what kind of follow-up?
- Which prospects need more information?
- Which prospects are Finished Leads?
- Which prospects are now disqualified?
- Which prospects were unavailable and should be called again?

Answering such questions will be easy because you will have given each prospect called a corresponding "OK" Code which tells you his or her status and exactly what he or she needs in terms of follow-up.

In the next section, we will examine how to use "OK" Codes in more detail.

## Key Terms to Remember

**Call-Cycle** – The time period in which you plan to call through your entire list once.

**"OK" Code** – A numeric code that classifies the outcome of every call.

**"OK" Field** – The data field in which "OK" Codes are entered, stored and sorted.

**"Last Call" Field** – The data field that keeps track of the last date a call was placed to a prospect in a call-cycle.

**Current "OK" Field** – The "OK" field that corresponds to the call cycle in which you are currently working.

**Current "Last Call" Field** – The "Last Call" field that corresponds to the call cycle in which you are currently working.

**[[Ascending Sort]]** – A data sort from "least to greatest" or from "oldest to newest."
