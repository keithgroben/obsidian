# 2.3 Databases for Prospecting

Defining Your Databases

You will need to make use of computer databases to successfully manage your prospecting efforts. I use as many as five different databases to manage my various activities.

1. **[[List Scrubbing Database]].** This is a large spreadsheet that is used for scrubbing or removing duplicates and other disqualified names from my primary list.

2. **Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM).** This database contains both my post-scrubbed primary list and my [[Plus List]]. This is the database from which I make calls and send individual permission-only emails.

3. **[[Client Service Database]].** Once a prospect becomes a client, they are "converted" to the Client Service Database which is used for servicing new and current customers. In some cases, a conversion feature (i.e. from Lead to Account) may be included with your CRM.

4. **Specialized [[Mailing Database]].** This is a database specifically designed to manage and print mailing lists. Importantly, it has the capacity to sort, generate and print barcodes, tray tags, and summaries for presorted-standard (bulk) mail.

5. **Do Not Call list.** This is a spreadsheet used to keep my in-house "Do Not Call" list. New prospect names from all sources must be compared against this list prior to being called. (See "Appendix A" on Telemarketing Legislation.)

[[List Scrubbing]]

The List Scrubbing database contains every listing in my universe. I use various tools within it to help me sort names into categories and remove unwanted names. Examples include, duplicates, in-house Do-Not-Call numbers, and other known disqualified listings.

A useful tool for list scrubbing is called "conditional formatting." It can be set to highlight the color of any duplicates by column. The goal of working with the List Scrubbing database is to end up with a call-ready list that can be exported to my CRM.

Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)

If you look online, you will find a wide array of vendors in the CRM space. Most provide templates for data fields and allow you to add custom fields for your specific business.

All CRMs can map, import, and export data as needed while you scrub and compile your list. Many CRM vendors can also securely store your data in the "cloud." This helps with backing up your data and safeguarding your information.

Establishing a Plus List Database

A [[Plus Listing]] is a prospect originating on a primary list who displays a minor, yet favorable difference between himself or herself and other general prospects on the primary list. This prospect is then "coded" or tagged (more on this later) for future reference. A "Plus List Database" is created by sorting or filtering these prospects from the primary list.

How to Upgrade Prospects to Your Plus List

The micro-skill of upgrading prospects to a Plus List is a bit of an art form and something that you will develop through activity over time. When considering which prospects to upgrade to your Plus List, ask yourself what makes one prospect more qualified than another? What are the subtle or circumstantial differences that make a prospect more likely to be a buyer?

Here are some upgrade-to-Plus List scenarios I use,

- A business prospect I call who gives me the contact name of the person in charge of buying what I sell.
- A prospect who is not interested currently, but with whom I have had a favorable conversation. Such connections are the seeds of building relationships over time.
- A prospect who indicates he or she may have future interest in what I sell.
- A prospect who asks me to send more information by mail, email, or fax.
- A prospect about which I have an intuitive hunch.
- A prospect I called for one product category who revealed – directly or by inference – that he or she is a prospect for a different product I sell. (i.e. Initial call: "Hamburger on Sale!" Prospect response: "I don't eat meat!" Follow-up: Send mailer on vegetarian cooking class.)
- A prospect I have met with or favorably made a presentation to in the past who did not end-up buying from me.
- In some special cases, the prospect may be a current customer who is also a prospect for a product or service I have not yet sold to him or her.

Qualifying Plus Listings Over Time

Both old and new Plus Listings exist together on the same Plus List. Over time, the information gathered about individual Plus Listings tends to improve. At any given time, information known about some Plus Listings may be much more detailed than the information known about others.

As a group, however, all Plus Listings,

- Are better qualified than general prospects on the primary list.
- Tend to become better and better qualified as time goes by.

Plus Listings with particularly defining characteristics may be filtered or queried out of the database from time to time for special opportunities. It can also happen that during either telephone calls or other research, information is collected that will disqualify a Plus Listing. When this occurs, the prospect's name will be marked as disqualified for future removal from the CRM database. I keep a list of disqualified names on the List Scrubbing database mentioned earlier.

Adding Custom Data Fields

In your CRM, be sure to include a field for "Call Back" or "Re-Contact" so you can enter long-term call back or re-contact dates. Such dates are only used in this system when prospects indicate definitive, long-term future re-contact dates (i.e. months away). Short-term call backs such as, "I'm busy this week, can you call me next week?" are generally sent One-Pagers rather than scheduled to be called back (more on this in Section 3).

Include in your database any specialized fields relating to your industry. Specialized fields may be used to filter and keep track of useful details you learn about your prospects such as the name of a current vendor, current model-type, model-year, contract renewal/termination date or other helpful information.

If you supplement this system with referred leads, you may wish to include a field called "Nominator" to help you remember from whom a referral originated. If you use different primary sources to create your primary list, it can also be helpful to include a field called "Source" so you know from which primary source each listing originated.

Key Terms to Remember

**List Scrubbing Database** – This is a large database in which listings from all sources are compared and screened prior to calling. Duplicates, disqualified names, Do-Not-Call names are filtered out here. The end goal is to create a call-ready list that can be exported to your CRM.

**Customer Relationship Management Database (CRM)** – This database contains both my post-scrubbed primary list and my Plus List. This is the database from which I make calls and send individual permission-only emails.

**Client Service Database** – A database used to service new and current customers. The client service database is generally kept separate from databases used for prospecting.

**Mailing Database** – A database set up on software specifically designed to manage mailing lists.

**Plus List** – A list of pre-screened prospects who have revealed themselves to be better-qualified prospects versus other prospects on a general primary list.

**Plus Listing** – An individual prospect on the Plus List. Such a prospect may be an old prospect or a new prospect. Plus Listings are known to be better qualified than prospects on a general primary list.
