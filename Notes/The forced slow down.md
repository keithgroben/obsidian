# The forced slow down

*Source: Notion — https://www.notion.so/2dd6abe00ba480b5ae7ff213bce04376*

In the era of Nintendo Power and 20th-century magazines, the medium forced you into a specific behavior that the internet has since "killed." Here is exactly what was lost:

## 1. The "Forced Slow-Down"

Unlike a website where you can open 50 tabs or scroll a mile in a minute, a physical magazine is a linear experience. You have to turn the page. You can't "search" for just the cheat codes without seeing the art, reading the letter from the editor, or noticing the community ads along the way. It forced you to go through the information slowly and sequentially.

## 2. Deep Immersion (The "Slow Reveal")

In those decades, discovery was a process, not an instant hit. You had to wait a month for the next issue. That waiting period created anticipation and depth. You didn't just "consume" the information; you lived with it. You studied the maps, reread the articles, and memorized the details because that was the only source you had.

## 3. The Filtered Funnel

Because printing and distribution were expensive, an editor had to decide what was "worthy" of the page. This acted as a high-quality filter. You went through it slowly because you knew someone had curated it specifically for you. The internet replaced that "Filter" with a "Firehose," which is why we feel overwhelmed today.

## 4. Tactile Memory

The physical act of holding the paper, smelling the ink, and flipping the pages engaged more of your senses. This is why you remember Nintendo Power so vividly — your brain was "anchored" to a physical object. Digital media is weightless and frictionless, so it passes through our brains without sticking.

## Why this matters for "The Wayfinder"

In 2026, The Wayfinder brings back that "Slow" feeling by:

- **Rejecting the Scroll:** You aren't giving them a feed; you're giving them a Dispatch.
- **Intentional Friction:** By sending a newsletter or a video that covers multiple "Columns" (Marketing, Bible, Culture), you are forcing the reader to slow down and see the connections between different parts of life.

You are essentially telling your audience: *"Stop running. Sit on the porch. Let's look at this together, one page at a time."*
