**Late 2001/Early 2002 (Age 22)**: Digitell Cellular - EARLY MARKETING RECOGNITION
- **Company**: Digitell Cellular (cellular retail company)
- **Owners**: Tim and Jim (brothers)
- **Location**: Mall kiosks and retail stores, Phoenix/Scottsdale area
- **Position**: Salesman
- **Age**: 22 years old
- **How He Got the Job**: Scott (manager) went to Phoenix First Bible school with Keith

**Sales Performance & Challenge:**
- **Goal**: 1 phone per day (5 per week), company would settle for 4 per week
- **Keith's Initial Performance**: 2-3 phones per week regularly
- **No Training**: "They just kind of expected you to talk people into it"
- **The Problem**: 80% bad sales conversations, 20% good - people would ask price and walk away

**Innovation #1 - Price Comparison Cards (Mall Kiosk):**
- **Created in Microsoft Word** (took a long time, didn't know how to use it well)
- **Card Contents**: Phone name, wireless carrier, "retail price" (higher), "sale price" (lower)
- **Pricing Strategy**: Used fact that salespeople could sell at any price for higher commission
  - Listed inflated "retail" price ($20-80 higher)
  - Listed actual price as "sale price"
- **Psychology**: Eliminated price-only shoppers, attracted serious buyers
- **Result**: Got extra 2 phones per week (from 2-3 to ~4 per week)
- **Pattern Recognition**: Learned to identify serious buyers vs. casual lookers by their behavior patterns
  - "Some people would just look out of curiosity and some people would look out of intention"
  - Started recognizing these patterns before engaging

**Company-Wide Adoption:**
- **Brad** (top manager/salesperson) saw cards, asked about them, said "these are great"
- **Jan** (Operations Manager) requested the Word template
- **Owners Tim and Jim** wanted them in ALL stores
- Became company standard - "literally my exact same template"
- Still in use a year later (with modified design)

**Innovation #2 - Merchandising (Scottsdale Pavilions Store):**
- **Store Situation**: Strip mall location, described as "slower store sales-wise"
- **Customer Pattern**: Sophisticated buyers already had phones, came in asking for:
  - Batteries, car chargers, antennas
  - Nokia lights, face plates, accessories
- **Initial Problem**: Had to say "no" for weeks - pegboards were bare
- **Solution**:
  - Learned from manager Scott that he could order products via fax
  - Started ordering what customers asked for
  - Ordered multiples (reasoning: if one person asks, others might too)
  - Built up inventory to fill empty pegboards
- **Result**:
  - Only sold 2-3 phones per week
  - But profit from product sales = equivalent of 5-6 phones per week
  - "Essentially able to keep that location going and in business"
- **Recognition**: "I actually did not get any recognition or bonus or promotion or reward or even acknowledgement for it at all"

**Failed Experiment - Flyers:**
- Tried putting flyers on cars and going door-to-door to businesses
- **Lesson Learned**: "You're not going to get anywhere with like 25 flyers... you have to do thousands"
- "That was a lesson of failure right there"

**Key Observations:**
- Top salespeople got to choose locations; Keith had to "make it work" at less desirable spots
- Turned "dead" mall location into better performer through innovation
- Women tend to do better in sales situations (Keith's observation)
- Started tracking behavioral patterns of potential customers

**Significance**:
- First documented marketing innovations in career
- Pattern recognition of customer psychology
- Understanding qualification vs. tire-kickers
- Creating scalable solutions (cards adopted company-wide)
- NOT the start of freelance career, but revealed marketing intuition
- Company later went out of business when carriers required exclusivity (sold Verizon only)

**THE TECHNOLOGY EVOLUTION - Constant Adaptation (2006-2019+):**

**Phase 1: Hand-Coded Era (2006-2008)**
- HTML and CSS by hand
- Dreamweaver for development
- Complete control but time-intensive

**Phase 2: Early WordPress Era (2007-2010)**
- Started with WordPress version 1.21.8
- Back then: "Nothing more than a blogging platform"
- Not yet a full site builder
- Now: "World's most popular site builder"
- Tried Joomla and Drupal: "Very complicated"

**Phase 3: Template Evolution (2008-2012)**
- **Problem**: "Trying to design everything by hand was difficult"
- **Client Reality**: People didn't want to pay for design, didn't have money, or didn't understand it would cost "more than double of just building a website"
- **Solution #1**: Free template libraries for HTML/CSS
- **Solution #2**: Buy templates for $5-15 (HTML/CSS)
- **Solution #3**: ThemeForest WordPress themes (game-changer)

**The New Workflow - Implementation Over Custom Design:**
- "Hey, you are my client, if I can show you about 3 templates, and we can work off of one of those"
- Went more into implementation and technical side
- Still could do custom design and would get hired for it
- But template approach became primary method
- "People really liked that they were getting a great design"

**The Wix Moment - Paradigm Shift:**
- Another freelancer: "I'm doing websites for people through Wix"
- Keith's reaction: "That's not really web design, because you're not doing it by hand"
- Freelancer: "Yeah, but you don't have to anymore"
- **The Click**: "That kind of clicked"
